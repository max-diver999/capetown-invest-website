## What is the GV2025 general valuation roll?

GV2025 is the City of Cape Town's mass revaluation of about 970,000 properties, restating each one's market value as at 1 July 2025, and it typically moved suburb values 20% to 40% above the previous roll. From 1 July 2026 that number, less the R620,000 exemption, is what your rates bill is calculated on.

The cycle ran on fixed dates that now define what owners can and cannot do:

| Milestone | Date |
| --- | --- |
| Valuation date (market value fixed) | 1 July 2025 |
| Roll certified | 30 January 2026 |
| Public inspection and objections | 20 February to 30 April 2026 |
| New values drive rates bills | 1 July 2026 |
| Roll remains in force | Through June 2029 |

Mass appraisal is statistical, not bespoke: the municipal valuer models suburbs from transaction data rather than inspecting each home, which is why individual entries can miss by 10% to 15% in either direction. Cape Town Invest treats the GV number as an input for rates arithmetic and never as evidence of achievable resale price, a distinction covered in the [prices-by-suburb data](/guides/cape-town-property-prices-by-suburb-2026/).

## How is your 2026/27 rates bill actually calculated?

The 2026/27 rates formula is municipal value minus the residential exemption, times the rate-in-rand. The adopted budget set the residential rate-in-rand near 0.0064, a 10.2% cut, and raised the rates-free portion to R620,000 for residential homes valued up to R8 million, both effective 1 July 2026 alongside the new values.

Worked examples across three price points, all modelled on the residential tariff:

| GV2025 value | Rateable balance after R620,000 | Modelled annual rates | Modelled monthly |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R2,000,000 | R1,380,000 | R8,830 | R736 |
| R3,000,000 | R2,380,000 | R15,230 | R1,270 |
| R5,000,000 | R4,380,000 | R28,030 | R2,336 |
| R10,000,000 | R9,380,000 (no exemption above R8m) | R64,000 | R5,333 |

Homes valued above R8 million lose the exemption entirely, which steepens the effective curve at the top end. Whether your bill rose in July depends on one comparison: if your valuation grew more than 10.2%, the tariff cut did not fully absorb it. The City projected a decrease or no change for about 60% of residential properties; the [rates and taxes guide](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) holds the full mechanics.

## Can you still object to your GV2025 valuation?

The objection window is closed: inspection and objections ran from 20 February to 30 April 2026, and the City accepts no late objections against the general roll, whatever the size of the increase, even one above 30%. What remains open depends entirely on whether you filed before the deadline.

The live routes now:

- **You objected in time:** the municipal valuer's decision arrives in writing, and from that notice you have a limited window to appeal to the independent Valuation Appeal Board. A compulsory review also applies where an objection decision changes a value by more than 10%.
- **You missed the window:** supplementary valuations remain available for clear errors, wrong category, wrong extent, new construction, subdivision or consolidation, each published with its own objection window during the cycle.
- **Everyone:** the next general valuation offers a full reset, with bills on the current roll running through June 2029.

Evidence standards decide the rest of the journey.

Our analysis of past valuation cycles says the appeal stage rewards evidence, not indignation, and the evidence that wins is narrow. Transacted sales from around 1 July 2025, the fixed valuation date, decide these disputes; current asking prices, portal estimates and the 2026 market are all irrelevant to what the property was worth on that day. An owner whose GV2025 entry implies a price 15% above any registered sale on the street that season has a case worth filing, and at a rates cost of 0.0064 per rand a R500,000 overvaluation is worth about R3,200 a year for 3 years, roughly R9,600 over the life of the roll. An owner who simply dislikes a higher bill does not have a case, and the Valuation Appeal Board dismisses that category quickly. Gather the comparables before the decision notice arrives, because the appeal window is short once it does.

## What did the 2026 court ruling change about your bill?

The April 2026 ruling of the Western Cape High Court means value-linked fixed charges are gone from your bill: the court declared the City's fixed water, sanitation and cleaning charges, calculated on property value bands, unlawful and ordered them removed within 3 months. The case reshaped the 2026/27 budget around the rates line.

Three consequences landed on owner cash flow, brought by the South African Property Owners' Association and AfriForum:

- **Cleaning charge scrapped:** the city-wide cleaning levy disappeared from bills entirely.
- **Meter, not value:** fixed water and sanitation charges reverted to water-meter size, decoupling them from GV2025.
- **Relief widened:** the City funded part of the gap through rates while raising the rates-free portion to R620,000.

Our analysis puts the combined effect on a R3 million home at a meaningful offset against the valuation increase, once the 2026/27 tariff rises of 4.5% on water and sanitation and 6.64% on electricity are counted, all worked through in the [utilities cost guide](/guides/cape-town-utilities-costs-owners-2026/). The precedent travels further than one budget: charges that scale with property value must be adopted as rates, through the rates process, not bolted onto the utility bill as tariffs.

## What does GV2025 mean when you buy or sell in Cape Town?

Every purchase now inherits a GV2025 value, a rates category and any unresolved dispute, and all three are due diligence items that cost R0 to check. On a R5,000,000 home the difference between a correct and inflated roll entry is worth thousands of rand a year for 3 years, and the account transfers with the property.

The pre-offer checks Cape Town Invest runs on every transaction:

1. **Roll entry:** confirm the GV2025 value and category match the property's actual use and extent.
2. **Account history:** 12 months of municipal statements, checking arrears and the rates category billed.
3. **Price-to-GV gap:** a purchase price far above municipal value flags a likely supplementary revaluation, and a higher bill, after transfer.
4. **Dispute status:** ask in writing whether any objection, appeal or supplementary process is pending on the property.

Insider tip: the rates clearance certificate required for transfer only proves the account is paid up; it says nothing about category or pending revaluation. A dedicated short-let billed as residential today can be reclassified under the draft 2026 letting by-law, and the [cost of buying guide](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-cape-town/) shows where that lands in the full acquisition stack alongside the running costs modelled in the [rental yield guide](/guides/cape-town-rental-yield-guide/).

## Which owners should act on GV2025 now?

Action depends on your position in the cycle, and in our analysis the honest first step for most owners is arithmetic, not paperwork. Recalculate the 2026/27 bill from the GV2025 value, the 0.0064 rate-in-rand and the R620,000 exemption, and only then decide whether any formal route repays the effort involved.

- **Objectors awaiting decisions:** diarise the appeal window the moment the valuer's decision arrives; the clock runs from the notice, not from July.
- **Owners with clear roll errors:** wrong category, wrong extent or a demolished structure justify a supplementary valuation request at any point in the cycle.
- **Foreign owners:** appoint your conveyancer or managing agent to receive municipal correspondence, because decision notices sent to an unwatched postal address burn the appeal window silently.
- **Buyers mid-transaction:** price the 2026/27 bill from the roll entry, not the seller's old statements, which reflect the pre-July tariff.

Owners above the R8 million line carry the sharpest exposure in the whole system, and the numbers explain why. A home valued at R8,000,000 exactly still enjoys the R620,000 exemption and pays on R7,380,000, roughly R47,200 a year at the 0.0064 rate, while a home valued at R8,100,000 loses the exemption entirely and pays on the full amount, about R51,800, so crossing the threshold by R100,000 of value adds over R4,500 a year for 3 years. Atlantic Seaboard stock concentrates precisely in this band, which makes the GV2025 entry worth professional attention there: a credible valuation report arguing R7.9 million against a roll entry of R8.2 million carries a rand value that dwarfs its fee. For that band the appeal evidence standard rewards transacted comparables from mid-2025 and professional reports over portal estimates, every time.
