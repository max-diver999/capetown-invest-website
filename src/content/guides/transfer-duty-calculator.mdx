## How transfer duty is actually calculated

On a progressive scale, not a flat percentage, and the distinction is worth several thousand rand of misunderstanding. Each band carries a fixed amount for everything below it plus a marginal rate on the slice above, so a property that crosses a band boundary by one rand pays the marginal rate on that one rand rather than a higher rate on the whole price.

Six bands run from nothing below R1,210,000 up to 13% on the portion above R13,310,000, and the [transfer duty guide](/guides/south-africa-transfer-duty-explained/) owns the full scale, the fixed amount attaching to each band, and what happens when the price includes movable property. The calculator above applies exactly that scale and shows which band your price lands in.

Four prices show what the progression does in practice, and the middle two are the ones that surprise buyers most.

| Purchase price | Which band it lands in | Duty | As a share of price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R1,200,000 | Below the threshold | Nothing | 0% |
| R1,300,000 | The 3% band | R2,700 | 0.2% |
| R3,000,000 | The 11% band, just inside it | R107,356 | 3.6% |
| R10,000,000 | The 11% band, well inside it | R877,356 | 8.8% |

The jump between rows two and three is the whole point of a progressive scale. Duty as a share of price more than doubles between R3,000,000 and R10,000,000 while the marginal rate stays at 11%, because the fixed amount attaching to the band is spread over a larger purchase.

Insider tip: duty is calculated on the purchase price or the fair market value, whichever SARS considers higher. A price agreed well below market on a family transfer does not reduce the duty, and the attorney is obliged to raise it.

## What the attorney and Deeds Office lines really are

Two separate costs that arrive together on the same statement. The transferring attorney's fee is a professional fee on a recommended guideline rather than a fixed tariff, so it varies by firm; the Deeds Office line is a disbursement covering the lodgement itself and the searches around it.

Expressed as a percentage the fee falls as the price rises, from roughly 1.15% to 1.4% in the R1,000,000 to R2,000,000 band down to roughly 0.62% to 0.84% between R5,000,000 and R10,000,000. That taper is the reason a calculator built on a flat percentage overstates the cost on expensive stock and understates it on cheap stock, and it is why the tool above interpolates between published anchor points instead. The [conveyancing fees guide](/guides/conveyancing-fees-cape-town/) owns the scale and sets out what each line covers.

Who carries which line is settled by convention rather than negotiated, and buyers routinely budget for the wrong half.

| Cost | Buyer | Seller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Transfer duty or VAT | Yes | No |
| Transferring attorney and Deeds Office | Yes | No |
| Bond registration and bank initiation | Yes, if financing | No |
| Estate agent commission | No | Yes |
| Compliance certificates | No | Yes |
| Bond cancellation on the existing loan | No | Yes |

One point of confusion worth settling early: the transferring attorney is normally nominated by the seller and paid by the buyer. They act for the transfer rather than for either party's negotiating position, and a buyer who wants their own advice on the offer needs a separate attorney for that.

## Why bond registration is a separate number

Because it is a separate transaction with a separate attorney, registering the bank's security rather than transferring the property. It scales with the bond amount rather than the purchase price, which means a larger deposit reduces two costs at once: the interest you will pay, and the registration fee itself.

A cash purchase avoids all of it. For a non-resident the arithmetic runs the other way: banks typically cap lending near 50% loan-to-value, so the bond is smaller than a local buyer's on the same property, the registration cost is lower, and the cash requirement is much higher. The [non-resident mortgage guide](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-cape-town/) sets out what the banks ask for.

## New build or resale changes which tax applies

Only one of the two ever applies. A new build bought directly from a VAT-registered developer carries 15% VAT and no transfer duty; a resale from an ordinary seller carries transfer duty and no VAT.

The practical trap is quotation rather than rate. Developers normally quote VAT-inclusive, and a price quoted excluding VAT raises the real cost by a sixth, which on a R4,000,000 apartment is R600,000. Get the VAT position in writing on the reservation form rather than at transfer, and set the calculator above to the developer option to see the duty line disappear. The [new developments hub](/guides/new-developments-cape-town-2026/) covers the rest of the off-plan sequence.

## What the calculator does not include

Four things that belong in a full purchase budget and are not transfer costs:

| Not in the calculator | When it lands | Who decides the amount |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Occupational rent | Only if you occupy before registration | Your offer to purchase |
| Rates and levy clearance | Before lodgement, apportioned later | The City and the body corporate |
| Your own inspection | Before the offer goes unconditional | You, and it is the cheapest money in the deal |
| Ongoing municipal rates | Every month from registration | The valuation roll, not the price you paid |

The last row is the one that outlives the transaction. Rates continue for as long as you own the property and are billed against the municipal valuation rather than the purchase price, which the [rates and taxes guide](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) works through with the arithmetic.
