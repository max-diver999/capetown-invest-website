## How long does property transfer take in South Africa?

Transfer from accepted offer to registration takes 8 to 12 weeks for a bonded Cape Town sale, and foreign buyers should plan on the same band as locals. The Deeds Office is the short part: 5 to 10 working days of examination. The long part is convergence, getting finance, clearances and certificates ready to lodge together.

| Phase | What happens | Typical duration |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1 to 2 | Offer signed, conveyancer appointed, FICA, bond application | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Weeks 2 to 5 | Bond approval and instruction, certificates, guarantees | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Weeks 3 to 6 | Rates clearance figures, payment, certificate | 10 to 14 working days |
| Weeks 4 to 7 | Transfer duty assessed and paid to SARS, receipt issued | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Weeks 6 to 10 | Documents signed, batch lodged at Deeds Office | On readiness |
| Weeks 7 to 12 | Examination and registration | 5 to 10 working days |

The bands overlap deliberately: in our analysis of transfer files, a good conveyancer runs every thread in parallel, and the calendar position of each phase depends on the slowest document, not the fastest. The [full selling process](/guides/selling-property-south-africa-non-resident/) and the [conveyancing fees guide](/guides/conveyancing-fees-cape-town/) cover who pays for which thread.

## Who does what in a South African transfer?

Three separate attorneys typically run one transfer, and the structure explains most delays. The transferring attorney, appointed by the seller and paid by the buyer, drives the deal; the bond attorney acts for the buyer's bank; the cancellation attorney acts for the seller's bank, and nothing lodges until all three are ready for the Deeds Office and its examination of 5 to 10 working days.

- **Transferring attorney:** drafts the deed of transfer, runs FICA on both parties, collects and pays transfer duty of up to 13% to SARS, obtains the rates clearance certificate and coordinates lodgement.
- **Bond registration attorney:** registers the buyer's new bond and issues the guarantees that will pay the price at registration.
- **Bond cancellation attorney:** obtains the seller's bank consent and cancels the existing bond, the thread most often started late.

Insider tip: ask the transferring attorney one question at the start, "what is your lodgement target date?", and then ask which document is currently the constraint every week. Files drift when nobody names the critical path; our analysis of delayed transfers shows the constraint is usually known to someone and communicated to no one, which is how an 8 to 12 week transfer quietly becomes 16 weeks.

## Which stages cause the biggest delays?

Five delay patterns account for most transfers that overrun the 8 to 12 week band, and every one of them is visible, and preventable, in week one. Foreign buyers and sellers hit the same five as locals, plus a courier cycle of their own covered below.

| Delay | Typical cost | Week-one prevention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unserved 90-day bond notice | Penalty interest, weeks of waiting | Written notice at mandate |
| Municipal arrears or disputes | Rates clearance stalls past 14 working days | Pull statement, settle early |
| Slow transfer duty payment | SARS receipt gates lodgement | Pay on assessment, immediately |
| Failed compliance certificates | Days to weeks of remediation | Pre-inspect before listing |
| Lost title deed | Replacement application, advertisement weeks | Locate the deed at mandate |

A worked example shows how the innocent version looks in practice. A Rondebosch sale signs in early March with a R2.9 million price and a bonded buyer: the buyer's bond approves in 3 weeks, certificates pass, and the file looks healthy. The seller's cancellation attorney, instructed in week four, discovers the original title deed is with a bank that archived it off-site after the bond was paid down years earlier, and retrieval plus the bank's consent adds 3 weeks to lodgement. Rates figures, requested in week five instead of week one, add another 10 working days of pure waiting. Registration lands in week fifteen instead of week ten: no crisis, no dispute, roughly 5 weeks lost to two documents nobody looked for until an attorney asked. The transfer that runs on time is the one where the boring documents were found in the first fortnight.

The two documents to find before anything else:

- **The original title deed:** in the seller's safe, the bank's archive or the previous conveyancer's strongroom; archive retrieval alone can run 1 to 3 weeks.
- **The municipal statement:** a clean account keeps the rates clearance on its 10 to 14 working day script; a disputed one stops it entirely.

## What extra steps hit non-resident buyers and sellers?

Foreign buyers and non-resident sellers add a documentation layer rather than a different process, and the calendar cost is 2 to 4 weeks when started late, close to zero when started at signature. The transfer itself runs the same 8 to 12 week pipeline regardless of where either party lives.

1. **Power of attorney round-trip:** documents signed abroad need notarisation, usually an apostille, and courier legs each way; the [remote buying guide](/guides/buy-cape-town-property-remotely/) prices the 2 to 4 week cycle.
2. **FICA on foreign documents:** certified passports, proof of foreign address and source-of-funds checks take longer than local verification; the [FICA guide](/guides/fica-requirements-foreign-property-buyers/) lists the exact pack.
3. **Seller-side tax mechanics:** above R2 million, the 7.5% to 15% section 35A withholding applies, and an NR03 directive with its 2 to 6 week SARS response belongs in week one, alongside the SARS clearance the bank needs before [proceeds leave the country](/guides/repatriating-property-sale-proceeds/).

Cape Town Invest plans every remote transaction backwards from lodgement: each of the three items above runs happily in parallel with the ordinary transfer, and each one added at the end extends the transfer by its full length.

## How can you compress the transfer timeline?

Compression is mostly document readiness for foreign buyers and locals alike, and the achievable gain is real: deals with complete files register in 6 to 8 weeks against the ordinary 8 to 12. Nothing legally shortens the Deeds Office examination of 5 to 10 working days, so every saved week comes from the preparation phases.

The levers that work:

- **Cash or pre-approved finance:** bond approval is the longest single thread; buyers with [pre-approval or cash](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-cape-town/) cut 2 to 4 weeks.
- **Certificates before listing:** pre-inspection converts remediation from a critical-path delay into background maintenance.
- **Rates figures in week one:** the 10 to 14 working day clearance starts on application, not on goodwill.
- **Transfer duty on assessment day:** the SARS receipt gates lodgement, and paying is the one step entirely in the buyer's control.
- **Weekly constraint check:** one email asking which document is the current blocker keeps three attorneys pointed at the same date.

None of these cost money beyond what the transaction already spends; our analysis of fast files shows they simply move the same spending earlier, which is the entire trick.

## What happens between lodgement and getting paid or getting keys?

Registration day is the legal pivot of the whole transaction for foreign buyers and locals alike: the Deeds Office registers the deed, ownership passes, the guarantees pay out and the transferring attorney disburses the price, all inside 1 business day. Everything before it is preparation, and everything after it is administration.

The last stretch in detail:

- **Lodgement to registration:** the linked batch from all three attorneys is examined over 5 to 10 working days in Cape Town, then comes up for registration; a rejection for a defect sends it back for correction and re-lodgement.
- **Money flow:** the seller's proceeds, less the bond settlement, commission and any section 35A withholding, pay out at registration; non-resident sellers then run the [repatriation clearance](/guides/repatriating-property-sale-proceeds/) to move funds offshore.
- **Occupation and risk:** possession follows the agreement, not the Deeds Office; where occupation precedes registration, occupational rent applies and the insurance handover date matters as much as the key handover.

Registration day itself rewards sellers who understand the payout order. The Deeds Office registers the linked batch, the bond guarantees are presented and paid the same day, and the transferring attorney's trust account disburses in sequence: bond settlement first, commission of 5% to 7.5% plus VAT next, any section 35A withholding of 7.5% to 15% held for SARS, and the balance to the seller, typically within 1 to 3 business days of registration. Buyers see the mirror image: the physical deed follows weeks later, but ownership, risk and the obligation to insure all moved at registration. A seller expecting R4.5 million should know the same-day figure is the net one, and a buyer expecting keys should re-read the occupation clause, because neither the Deeds Office nor the attorneys hand over either.

Our analysis of buyer files puts the most common post-registration surprise at exactly none of the legal steps: it is the utilities account, which should be opened in the buyer's name against the registered deed in the first week of ownership.
