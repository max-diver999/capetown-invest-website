Quick answer: a non-resident selling South African property faces four moving parts: section 35A withholding of 7.5% to 15% on any price above R2 million, capital gains tax on the profit with the withholding credited back, seller costs of roughly 5% to 7.5% agent commission plus R4,000 to R10,000 in compliance certificates, and, since late 2025, a SARS clearance step before the bank will send proceeds abroad. The transfer itself runs 8 to 12 weeks from accepted offer to registration.

## What does the full non-resident sale process look like?

The full sale runs on four parallel tracks, and sellers who work them in sequence add months to the money arriving offshore. Cape Town Invest maps the process as one pipeline: sale, tax, costs and repatriation, with the 8 to 12 week transfer as the spine that everything else must match.

| Stage | What happens | Typical timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mandate and marketing | Agent appointed, commission of 5% to 7.5% plus VAT agreed | 2 to 12 weeks to a signed offer |
| Compliance certificates | Electrical, water, beetle, gas, fence inspections | 1 to 2 weeks, R4,000 to R10,000 |
| Transfer process | Conveyancer, rates clearance, SARS receipts, Deeds Office | 8 to 12 weeks |
| Section 35A withholding | 7.5% to 15% withheld above R2 million, paid to SARS in 21 days | At registration |
| Repatriation | Bank remits after SARS clearance (AIT PIN or letter) | 1 to 6 weeks after registration |

Foreign sellers hold one structural advantage: everything on this list can be run remotely under a properly executed power of attorney, and the [remote transaction mechanics](/guides/buy-cape-town-property-remotely/) apply to selling exactly as they do to buying, in reverse.

## How does section 35A withholding hit your sale?

Section 35A requires the buyer, through the conveyancer, to withhold part of your price the moment two conditions meet: you are a non-resident, and the price exceeds R2 million. The rates are 7.5% for an individual, 10% for a company and 15% for a trust, paid to SARS within 21 days of registration.

The mechanics every seller should know before signing:

- **The trigger is residency, not citizenship:** a South African passport holder who is tax non-resident is caught; a foreign national who is SA tax resident is not.
- **Withholding is an advance, not the tax:** SARS credits it against your assessed capital gains liability and refunds any excess after your return.
- **A directive can shrink it:** form NR03 asks SARS to reduce or waive the withholding where the actual gain is small, and it must be lodged before registration.
- **Advisers carry duties:** the agent and conveyancer must warn the buyer in writing that section 35A may apply, on pain of personal liability.

The full worked example, the refund lag and the directive strategy sit in the dedicated [section 35A guide](/guides/section-35a-withholding-tax-explained/); on a R4.5 million individual sale the withholding alone is R337,500, which is why the directive matters.

## What does capital gains tax look like for a non-resident seller?

Capital gains tax is the real liability the withholding merely prepays, and for a non-resident individual it means 40% of the gain lands in taxable income at marginal rates, an effective charge of up to 18% of the profit. Companies and trusts include 80%, pushing effective rates to roughly 21.6% and 36%.

A worked example shows the full stack on a typical exit. An individual non-resident bought a Sea Point flat for R2.8 million in 2019, sells for R4.5 million in 2026, and spent R200,000 on capitalised improvements: the gain is R1.5 million, the R40,000 annual exclusion trims it to R1.46 million, 40% inclusion puts R584,000 into taxable income, and at a 45% marginal rate the CGT is about R262,800. Section 35A meanwhile withheld R337,500 at transfer, 7.5% of the full price, so this seller is owed roughly R74,700 back and waits for assessment to recover it, unless an NR03 directive matched the withholding to the real liability upfront. Base cost records decide everything: the [capital gains tax guide](/guides/south-africa-capital-gains-tax-property/) lists what SARS accepts, and foreign sellers who kept invoices from 7 years ago collect the reward now.

What counts into base cost, and therefore out of the taxable gain:

- **Acquisition costs:** the price, transfer duty, conveyancing fees and any VAT paid on purchase.
- **Capital improvements:** renovations and additions with invoices, not repairs or maintenance.
- **Disposal costs:** agent commission, compliance certificates and advertising for the sale.

Companies and trusts run the same computation at an 80% inclusion rate, which is why holding structure belongs in the purchase decision rather than the sale.

## What does selling actually cost you as the seller?

Seller costs in a South African sale cluster into five lines, and on a R4.5 million Cape Town sale they typically absorb R350,000 to R450,000 before tax. Commission dominates: 5% to 7.5% plus 15% VAT is the prevailing band, negotiable hardest on stock the agent expects to move fast.

| Cost line | Typical figure on R4.5 million |
| --- | --- |
| Agent commission at 5% to 7.5% plus VAT | R258,750 to R388,125 |
| Compliance certificates and inspections | R4,000 to R10,000 plus remediation |
| Bond cancellation attorney | R4,500 to R7,000 |
| Rates clearance advance to the City | 2 to 4 months of rates prepaid |
| Courier, notarisation, bank charges | R3,000 to R8,000 for remote sellers |

Two traps repeat in our analysis of seller files. Bond cancellation carries a notice period, commonly 90 days, and banks charge penalty interest when the sale registers earlier than the notice you gave, so notify your bank the day you mandate the agent. And remediation is open-ended: certificates cost thousands, but the rewiring or plumbing work a failed inspection triggers can cost tens of thousands, priced in the [full seller cost guide](/guides/cost-of-selling-compliance-certificates/).

## How do you move the proceeds out of South Africa?

Repatriation of sale proceeds now requires SARS clearance before the bank remits, and the rule reaches all foreign buyers who sell. An authorised dealer must verify tax compliance via an Approval for International Transfer PIN or a Manual Letter of Compliance, and a clean file moves the money offshore within 1 to 6 weeks of registration.

The clean path has three components, and missing any one strands the money in rand:

1. **The inward record:** proof of how your original purchase funds entered South Africa through an authorised dealer, created at purchase and irreplaceable later.
2. **The sale file:** agreement, conveyancer's final statement and proof of section 35A payment, showing the rand amount is what it claims to be.
3. **The SARS clearance:** the AIT PIN or compliance letter, confirming no outstanding returns or debt block the transfer.

Speed of repatriation tracks paperwork quality more than bank policy. Sellers with a complete file, meaning the inward transfer record, the sale agreement, the conveyancer's final statement and proof that the 7.5% to 15% section 35A amount reached SARS inside its 21 days, typically see proceeds leave within 1 to 2 weeks of registration. Sellers reconstructing an inward record from an old purchase, or registering with SARS retroactively to obtain the compliance letter, measure the same step in 2 to 6 months, during which rand proceeds that often run R3 million to R5 million sit exposed to currency drift. Cape Town Invest treats the SARS thread as day-one work: our analysis of delayed exits puts missing inward records behind most of the long tails, so the thread opens when the mandate is signed, not when the money is needed. The [repatriation guide](/guides/repatriating-property-sale-proceeds/) walks each document in order.

None of this restricts how much you can take out: the clearance verifies tax compliance, not an amount, and documented sale proceeds leave in full.

## When is the right time for a non-resident to sell?

Timing a non-resident exit is a three-variable decision: the property market, the rand, and your tax position, and the three rarely peak together. Western Cape prices carried 2026 momentum with forecasts of 7.4% to 9.3% growth, while the rand's long-run drift means the conversion date moves your home-currency result as much as the sale price does.

Our analysis of seller outcomes puts process quality above market timing in rand terms: the spread between a well-run and badly-run exit, directive lodged, certificates pre-inspected, SARS thread opened early, routinely exceeds 2% of the price, which is most of a year's capital growth. The decision list that matters:

- **Gain position:** small gain or loss argues for an NR03 directive before registration, releasing cash 6 to 12 months earlier.
- **Currency view:** agree with your FX provider whether to convert at registration or hold rand briefly; leaving it to default timing is a decision too.
- **Buyer profile:** cash buyers cut 2 to 4 weeks of bond risk from the 8 to 12 week spine.
- **Next step:** reinvesting locally changes everything, since staying in the market defers the entire exit stack.

Insider tip: request your rates account balance and a pre-inspection of the electrical installation before listing. The two slowest documents in a Cape Town transfer are rates clearance and a failed certificate remediation, and both can be started the week you decide to sell rather than the week an offer lands.

## Seller scenarios for non-resident exits

| Seller profile | Priority thread | Decision framework |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First-time non-resident seller | Inward record plus section 35A | Parallel SARS and conveyancer from mandate |
| Repeat seller with clean files | NR03 directive | Lodge if withholding exceeds expected CGT |
| Seller reinvesting locally | Defer repatriation | Exchange control still applies to new purchase |
| Trust or company seller | 15% withholding | Model trust CGT before accepting offer |

Scenario one: seller waits for "the right market moment" but ignores rand exposure. Registration happens on a weak rand day; proceeds convert 4% below budget. Scenario two: cash buyer offers 3% less but closes in six weeks. Process quality savings on certificates and clearance often beat holding for a higher bonded offer.

The decision framework mirrors the four-track sale: certificates, rates, section 35A, repatriation. Weakness in any track adds weeks. Sellers who open the SARS thread when they appoint the agent, not when they receive proceeds, routinely cut total exit time by a third.

If you plan to repurchase elsewhere in South Africa rather than remit, say so early. Reinvestment within the exchange control framework uses different forms but still needs SARS compliance on the sale leg. Mixing reinvestment with offshore remittance without a clear split confuses both the bank and SARS.

A first-time non-resident seller often underestimates how much stays in trust on registration day. On a R5.5 million Sea Point sale with R1.2 million bond, commission near R385,000 including VAT and section 35A withholding at 7.5% of R5.5 million, roughly R412,500, the seller's same-day net can be near R3.5 million before repatriation clearance even begins. Budget living and reinvestment plans on net, not headline price.

Selling from abroad without flying in is normal, but the power of attorney must track the exact buyer and property. Generic POA templates get rejected and cost another courier cycle. Have the conveyancer draft the document, sign at a notary who understands South African property deeds, and apostille where required. That step belongs in week one of the mandate alongside certificate pre-inspection, not after you accept an offer.

If the buyer is bonded and your sale is clean, a conditional offer with a realistic bond deadline protects you from open-ended delay. Non-resident sellers often accept the first bid without a date-certain registration clause, then carry rates, insurance and levy for extra months while the buyer's bank stalls.

Disclose non-resident status in the mandate, not only at conveyancing. Agents who learn late must rewrite compliance steps and may miss the NR03 window that would have improved your registration-day cash. Written residency disclosure in the mandate also protects you if the buyer's bank queries withholding late on registration day itself.
