## What do Cape Town Airbnb listings actually earn in 2026?

The citywide baseline is a median daily rate of about R1,719 at occupancy near 71%, measured across roughly 17,889 active listings in early 2026, with the average host earning about R451,000 for the year. Cape Town Invest treats these Airbtics baselines as the bar every suburb either beats or misses.

The distribution matters more than the average:

- **Top decile:** Camps Bay and Clifton villas clear R4,500 a night in season and carry the bulk of December earnings.
- **Core belt:** Sea Point, Green Point, the City Bowl and De Waterkant run R1,600 to R2,900 a night at 71% to 75% occupancy.
- **Value belt:** Woodstock, Observatory, Century City and Blouberg trade below R1,500 a night but from far cheaper stock.

Seasonality shapes all three tiers: the November to March peak can carry half the year's revenue, so annual occupancy of 71% blends a near-full summer with a slow winter. A listing that cannot survive its first winter on cash flow is underpriced on risk, not clever on yield.

## How do Airbnb yields compare suburb by suburb?

Modelled gross short-let yield is annual platform revenue divided by purchase price, and on that measure Cape Town's mid-market beats its trophy coast. The table below combines Airbtics and AirROI rate and occupancy bands with our own entry-price research from the [prices-by-suburb table](/guides/cape-town-property-prices-by-suburb-2026/); every figure is modelled.

| Suburb node | Entry price (1-bed) | Modelled ADR | Occupancy band | Modelled gross revenue/yr | Modelled gross STR yield |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Sea Point | R3.5 million | R1,800 | 71% to 75% | R479,000 | 13.7% |
| Green Point | R3.2 million | R1,700 | 71% to 75% | R452,000 | 14.1% |
| De Waterkant / City Bowl | R2.8 million | R2,000 | 71% to 75% | R532,000 | 19.0% |
| Woodstock / Observatory | R1.9 million | R1,100 | 65% to 70% | R271,000 | 14.3% |
| Century City | R2.2 million | R1,300 | 65% to 70% | R320,000 | 14.5% |
| Blouberg | R1.8 million | R1,500 | 60% to 68% | R350,000 | 19.4% |
| Camps Bay (apartment) | R6.5 million | R4,500 | 60% to 66% | R1,034,000 | 15.9% |

Read the last column with discipline: gross means before management at 20% to 25%, cleaning, utilities, levies, rates and the winter trough. Net short-let yields in the core belt typically land at half to two-thirds of gross once a full cost stack is applied, which is the arithmetic worked through in the [Airbnb investment guide](/guides/airbnb-investment-cape-town-guide/).

## Why does Camps Bay earn the most but yield less than Sea Point?

Camps Bay is the revenue champion at roughly R4,500 a night, and a four-bedroom villa at R5,000 a night with 70% occupancy models near R105,000 a month, yet its yield trails the mid-market. Entry prices explain the gap: trophy stock starts around R6.5 million for apartments.

A worked example makes the trade concrete. One Camps Bay apartment at R6.5 million modelling R1,034,000 of gross revenue returns 15.9% before costs, while the same capital buys two De Waterkant lofts at R2.8 million each, modelling a combined R1,064,000 from R5.6 million deployed, a 19% modelled gross yield with two review streams and two calendars instead of one. The single trophy asset wins on capital preservation, resale depth and December pricing power, when R4,500 nights stack into R135,000 months. The pair wins on cash flow, diversification and refurbishment flexibility, since one unit can trade while the other renovates. Both paths fail identically if a body corporate bans nightly letting, which is why the conduct-rule check precedes any of this arithmetic. All figures are modelled from platform bands, not audited accounts.

Villas widen the gap further, since entry prices above R20 million meet the same nightly ceiling.

Who each side of the trade suits:

- **Income-first foreign buyers:** the mid-market pair, with the [De Waterkant](/areas/de-waterkant-property-investment/) loft as the template.
- **Wealth-parking buyers with summer use:** the [Camps Bay strip](/areas/camps-bay-property-investment/), priced for scarcity rather than yield.
- **First-time short-let owners:** one mid-market unit before any portfolio, because management intensity scales with calendars.

## What costs does the gross yield hide?

Gross-to-net erosion is the most underestimated line in Cape Town short-letting, and it typically consumes a third to half of platform revenue. Management alone runs 20% to 25% of takings for a full-service operator, before cleaning, linen, utilities, levies, rates, insurance and platform fees touch the remainder.

The recurring stack on a R3 million Sea Point one-bedroom, modelled annually:

| Cost line | Modelled annual figure |
| --- | --- |
| Management at 20% to 25% of R479,000 | R96,000 to R120,000 |
| Levies and municipal rates | R45,000 to R60,000 |
| Cleaning, linen, consumables | R30,000 to R40,000 |
| Utilities, fibre, insurance uplift | R25,000 to R35,000 |
| Maintenance and replacement reserve | R15,000 to R25,000 |

Net of the full stack, the 13.7% gross models closer to 7% to 8% before tax, still ahead of long-let returns in the same buildings but no longer a different asset class. SARS then taxes the net profit whether you are resident or not, and the [short-term rental rules guide](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-cape-town/) covers the compliance layers that sit around the income.

## How does the 2026 by-law change the yield table?

The draft Short-Term Letting By-law published in August 2026 adds two costs to every dedicated short-let in the table above: registration compliance and commercial property rates. Commercial rating removes the R620,000 residential rates-free portion and applies a higher rate-in-rand, a swing that can exceed R15,000 a year on a R3 million flat.

Applied across the table, the by-law's arithmetic is regressive: a R15,000 rates swing costs a Blouberg listing at R350,000 of modelled revenue about 4.3% of takings, while the same swing costs a Camps Bay operation at R1,034,000 barely 1.5%. Cheaper stock feels the change hardest, and the under-50% room-night carve-out quietly favours hybrid holiday-home owners over pure investment listings at the value end, because a home let 20 weeks a year keeps its residential rating and its R620,000 exemption. Our analysis is that none of this kills the model; it moves the breakeven roughly half a yield point and rewards buyers who price it in against sellers who have not. Full registration mechanics, penalties and the enforcement timeline sit in the [by-law tracker](/guides/cape-town-str-bylaw-2026-registration/).

The registration duty itself is universal, with comments open until 5 October 2026.

What changes for each tier:

- **Core belt dedicated listings:** commercial rates plus registration, about 0.5 points of gross yield.
- **Value belt dedicated listings:** the same rand cost against smaller revenue, up to a full point.
- **Hybrid under-50% owners:** registration only, with the [hybrid model](/guides/airbnb-investment-cape-town-guide/) untouched on rates.

## Which suburb fits which Airbnb strategy?

Suburb choice is strategy choice, and the yield table sorts cleanly into four buyer intents once the by-law and the cost stack are priced in. Foreign buyers should match the node to the job the property must do over a 5 to 10 year hold, not to the highest number in any single column.

1. **Maximum cash flow per rand:** De Waterkant and City Bowl lofts, and Blouberg at the value end, model gross yields near 19% before costs.
2. **Balance of income and resale depth:** Sea Point and Green Point one-bedrooms pair 13% to 14% modelled gross with the deepest buyer pool at exit.
3. **Revenue scale and capital preservation:** Camps Bay earns the city's highest absolute income and holds value, at the price of yield.
4. **Lowest entry ticket:** Woodstock and Observatory get you into the market under R2 million with student and medical long-let fallbacks.

Insider tip: before any offer, pull the building's last two AGM minutes and registered conduct rules. Cape Town Invest has seen more short-let plans die to a 75% special resolution passed after purchase than to any market downturn, and schemes along the Sea Point strip vote on nightly-letting restrictions every season.
