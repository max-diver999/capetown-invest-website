## Can a body corporate ban Airbnb in a sectional title scheme?

A body corporate's power to ban Airbnb is settled law, not a grey area. Section 10 of the Sectional Titles Schemes Management Act allows a scheme to make conduct rules about the use of sections, and in our review of scheme disputes a minimum lease period of 6 months is the standard rule that ends nightly letting for foreign buyers and residents alike.

The requirements for a valid ban stack up as follows:

- **Special resolution:** at least 75% of votes by value and by number must support the amendment.
- **Reasonableness:** section 10(3) requires the rule to be reasonable and to apply equally to all owners.
- **CSOS approval:** the Community Schemes Ombud Service must examine and certify the rule before it binds anyone.
- **Proper process:** notice, quorum and voting formalities at the general meeting must be correct, or the rule is challengeable.

A rule that fails any leg can be attacked through CSOS adjudication, which costs a fraction of litigation. A rule that passes all four ends the debate, and the income.

## What did the Paddock case actually decide?

The Paddock case is the decision that settled the question. The body corporate of The Paddock, a sectional title scheme in Sandown, amended its conduct rules to prohibit leases shorter than 6 months after residents complained about security risks and guest behaviour tied to a unit run on Airbnb, and the High Court upheld the rule in January 2021.

The judgment matters to buyers for three reasons, and Cape Town Invest reads it as the controlling precedent for every scheme in the country. First, the court confirmed that short-term letting is a use of a section that conduct rules may regulate, so a ban is not an unlawful interference with ownership. Second, the rule bound the owner whose established Airbnb business it destroyed, which means an operating history creates no protected right to continue. Third, the court accepted security and nuisance complaints as a rational basis, a threshold most schemes with 1 problem listing can meet. An owner who bought specifically to short-let, with 3 years of reviews and a full calendar of bookings at any nightly rate, holds no veto in the end: the arithmetic is 75% of votes, and everything else is commentary.

What the precedent means in practice:

- **Use is regulable:** short-term letting is a section use that conduct rules may lawfully restrict.
- **No grandfathering:** an operating history creates no protected right to continue.
- **Low threshold:** documented security or nuisance complaints satisfy the rational-basis test.

Verify the rule status before transfer, not after; the precedent leaves nothing to argue once a valid rule is registered.

## How does a scheme actually pass a short-let ban?

The procedure runs from a tabled motion to a CSOS certificate, and each step is a checkpoint a buyer can monitor. A scheme typically requires 3 to 6 months to move from first complaint to registered rule, which is exactly the window in which a well-advised buyer walks away or reprices.

| Step | What happens | Typical timing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Motion tabled | Trustees or owners propose a conduct rule amendment | AGM or special general meeting |
| Notice issued | Written notice with the rule text goes to all owners | 14 to 30 days before the meeting |
| Special resolution | 75% by value and number, in person or round-robin | Meeting day |
| CSOS submission | Scheme files the amendment for approval | Within weeks of the vote |
| Certification | CSOS tests reasonableness and registers the rule | 1 to 3 months in practice |

Insider tip: the tell is almost never the rule itself but the AGM minutes 1 cycle earlier. Complaints about noise, key handovers and strangers in the lift appear a full season before any motion, and Cape Town Invest treats 2 consecutive sets of minutes with guest complaints as a live ban risk priced into the offer.

## What can owners do when a ban is proposed or passed?

Owners facing a proposed ban hold real but narrow options, and the strength of each depends on timing. Before the vote the fight is arithmetic: a proposer needs 75% by value and by number, so owners controlling just over 25% of either measure, foreign buyers included, can block the resolution outright.

After a rule passes, the remaining routes are:

1. **CSOS scrutiny:** argue during certification that the rule is unreasonable or unequally applied, for example if trustees run their own short-lets.
2. **CSOS adjudication:** dispute a registered rule's validity or its application to your unit through the ombud's process, at a filing cost far below litigation.
3. **Negotiated phase-out:** schemes commonly allow confirmed bookings to run off over 3 to 6 months; get the transition recorded in the minutes.
4. **Model change:** leases of 6 months and longer sit outside most bans, and the [long-term rental market](/guides/long-term-rental-cape-town-guide/) in Cape Town remains undersupplied.

A worked example from our review of scheme disputes shows why the arithmetic dominates. An investor holding 2 of 40 units controls about 5% by number, nowhere near a blocking position, and needs a coalition representing at least 9 more units to push the opposition past 25% and defeat a special resolution. In a typical Sea Point block where 60% of units belong to investors, assembling that coalition takes 3 phone calls; in an owner-occupied Gardens building where investors hold 8 units out of 40, it is arithmetically impossible without converting resident votes. The same count works in reverse before purchase: ask the managing agent for the ownership mix, estimate the investor share, and you can price the ban risk within 10 minutes. Count votes before counting on any legal remedy, because CSOS will not save a model that 75% of the scheme wants gone.

## How do you check a building before you buy for Airbnb?

Pre-purchase screening is a document exercise that costs nothing and beats every legal remedy available after transfer. The registered conduct rules and 2 years of meeting minutes reveal both the current position and the direction of travel, and foreign buyers can obtain every item remotely through the conveyancer.

The screening list Cape Town Invest applies before any short-let purchase:

- **CSOS-registered conduct rules:** read the actual registered text for minimum lease periods and guest provisions, not an agent summary.
- **AGM and SGM minutes, last 2 years:** look for guest complaints, tabled motions and levy disputes that signal an owner-occupier majority organising.
- **Ownership mix:** ask the managing agent what share of units is owner-occupied; blocks above roughly 50% owner occupation pass bans, investor blocks rarely do.
- **Written confirmation:** have the managing agent confirm in writing that no short-let rule amendment is tabled or pending CSOS approval.

The wider document pack, levies, reserves and maintenance plans, is covered in the [body corporate due diligence guide](/guides/body-corporate-due-diligence-cape-town/). Rules are the layer that kills the income; finances are the layer that kills the yield.

## How do conduct rules interact with Cape Town's 2026 by-law?

Conduct rules and the City's draft by-law operate as independent gates, and our analysis is that a listing must clear both to earn a single rand. The draft published in August 2026 requires every listed property to register and puts dedicated short-lets on commercial rates, stripping the R620,000 residential exemption, while granting no letting rights inside any scheme.

The compliance order now reads:

1. **Scheme conduct rules:** the gate that can close by 75% vote at any AGM.
2. **City registration:** number displayed on every listing once the by-law commences.
3. **Insurance and SARS:** guest cover arranged and rental income declared.

Order matters because the layers fail differently. A registered listing with a City number can still be shut down by a 75% special resolution the following season, while a scheme-approved listing that ignores registration faces fines of up to 6 months imprisonment under the draft's penalty clause once it commences. The [by-law tracker](/guides/cape-town-str-bylaw-2026-registration/) follows the City layer, the [short-term rental rules guide](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-cape-town/) maps all the layers together, and the suburb arithmetic lives in the [Airbnb yields table](/guides/airbnb-yields-by-suburb-cape-town/). Underwrite on the layer most likely to move: for sectional title, that is the scheme, not the City.
