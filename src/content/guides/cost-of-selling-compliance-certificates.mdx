## What does it cost to sell a house in Cape Town?

Selling costs in Cape Town typically absorb 6% to 9% of the price, and commission is two thirds of it. Cape Town Invest budgets five lines for every exit: commission, certificates, bond cancellation, the rates clearance advance and transaction logistics, with tax handled separately because withholding is a prepayment, not a cost.

| Cost line | On R3,000,000 | On R4,500,000 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Commission at 5% to 7.5% plus VAT | R172,500 to R258,750 | R258,750 to R388,125 |
| Certificates and inspections | R4,000 to R10,000 | R4,000 to R10,000 |
| Bond cancellation attorney | R4,500 to R7,000 | R4,500 to R7,000 |
| Rates clearance advance | 2 to 4 months of charges | 2 to 4 months of charges |
| Courier, notarisation, bank fees | R3,000 to R8,000 remote | R3,000 to R8,000 remote |

The buyer pays transfer duty and the transfer attorney; the seller's conveyancing exposure is the bond cancellation side, covered with the whole fee architecture in the [conveyancing fees guide](/guides/conveyancing-fees-cape-town/). Non-resident sellers should read this stack alongside the [four-track sale process](/guides/selling-property-south-africa-non-resident/), where these costs are one track of four.

## Which compliance certificates do you need and what do they cost?

Compliance certificates are the seller's statutory and contractual homework, and the set typically costs R4,000 to R10,000 to obtain. The electrical certificate is compulsory nationally, the water installation certificate is a City of Cape Town by-law requirement, and the beetle certificate is a Western Cape convention written into most sale agreements.

| Certificate | Requirement | Typical cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Electrical certificate of compliance | National law, under 2 years old | R850 to R1,500 |
| Water installation certificate | City of Cape Town by-law | Plumber call-out rates |
| Beetle (wood borer) certificate | Western Cape sale convention | About R1,800 |
| Gas certificate of conformity | Where a gas installation exists | About R800 |
| Electric fence system certificate | Where an electric fence exists | About R1,800 |

Certificates gate the transfer, not just the price: conveyancers will not lodge without the set the agreement requires, which is one of the quiet ways an [8 to 12 week transfer](/guides/property-transfer-timeline-delays/) loses weeks. Our analysis of delayed files puts certificate remediation among the top three causes, and the fix is cheap: pre-inspect before listing, and the work happens off the critical path.

A worked example shows why the inspection fee is the wrong number to optimise. A Claremont seller lists a 1970s house, and the electrical inspection after offer acceptance finds an aged distribution board, unearthed plugs, and a non-compliant geyser connection: R38,000 of remediation on a certificate that cost R1,200 to attempt. The work takes 2 weeks to schedule and complete, the certificate follows, and the transfer slips by roughly 3 weeks because the buyer's bank would not proceed without the compliant certificate in the file. The same seller, pre-inspecting for under R2,000 in the week the mandate was signed, would have done identical work with zero timeline cost and better contractor pricing, because nothing negotiates worse than a seller with a transfer waiting on one document.

## How much does the agent take and can you negotiate it?

Agent commission in Cape Town runs 5% to 7.5% plus 15% VAT, paid by the seller out of proceeds at registration. On a R4.5 million sale the spread between those rates is R129,375 including VAT, which makes the mandate conversation the highest-value hour in the entire sale.

What actually moves the number:

- **Stock the agent wants:** well-priced property in a moving segment earns the sharpest rate, because the agent's risk of a long, expensive marketing period is low.
- **Tiered structures:** a base rate to an agreed target price with a higher share above it aligns the agent with your ceiling rather than a quick close.
- **Sole versus open mandates:** sole mandates trade exclusivity for a lower rate and more marketing spend; open mandates pay full rate to whoever closes.
- **What is included:** professional photography, floor plans and portal placement belong in the mandate, not on top of it.

Insider tip: negotiate the commission before naming your asking price, not after. Agents calibrate effort to the mandate economics, and a rate agreed against a realistic valuation survives the marketing period intact, while a rate squeezed after an inflated valuation tends to resurface as pressure to accept the first offer.

## What do bond cancellation and rates clearance really cost?

Bond cancellation and rates clearance are the two seller costs with timing traps attached, and both are cheaper the earlier they start. The cancellation attorney charges R4,500 to R7,000, and the City's rates clearance certificate requires prepaying 2 to 4 months of municipal charges before it is issued in 10 to 14 working days.

The traps, and the free fixes:

1. **Penalty interest on the bond:** loan agreements commonly require 90 days of notice before cancellation; register earlier without notice given, and the bank charges penalty interest on the shortfall. Notify in writing the day you mandate an agent, which costs nothing.
2. **The clearance advance:** the City requires payment beyond your current account before issuing the certificate, and refunds the surplus after registration. Budget the cash flow: on a R4.5 million home the [rates](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) line alone runs about R2,069 a month, and with water, refuse and sewerage on top the municipal account commonly reaches R5,000, so the advance alone is R10,000 to R20,000.
3. **Old municipal debt:** arrears surface at clearance stage and stop the certificate entirely; pull a statement at mandate and dispute early, because a contested balance takes far longer than 14 working days to resolve.

Penalty interest is the cost sellers most often pay without noticing, and our analysis of exit files shows why. A R2.5 million bond at prime, currently around 10.5%, accrues roughly R21,900 a month in interest; where a sale registers 2 months inside an unserved 90-day notice period, the bank recovers the shortfall as penalty interest, and the seller donates over R40,000 for skipping a free letter. The rates clearance advance behaves the same way: the City holds 2 to 4 months of prepaid charges, R10,000 to R20,000 on a R4.5 million home, and refunds the surplus only after registration. Neither number appears in the mandate conversation, both are pure timing, and both are settled by two actions in week one: written notice to the bank and a clearance application backed by a clean municipal statement.

## What extra costs hit non-resident sellers?

Non-resident sellers add logistics and tax mechanics on top of the standard stack, and the cash items are modest: R3,000 to R8,000 in notarisation, apostille and courier costs for documents signed abroad, plus bank charges on the outbound transfer. The large number, the section 35A withholding, is a tax prepayment rather than a cost.

- **Power of attorney round-trip:** signing from abroad means notarised, often apostilled documents and a 2 to 4 week courier cycle, priced and planned in the [remote transaction guide](/guides/buy-cape-town-property-remotely/).
- **Section 35A withholding:** 7.5% to 15% of the price above R2 million goes to SARS at registration, credited against your capital gains tax; the [section 35A guide](/guides/section-35a-withholding-tax-explained/) covers the NR03 directive that shrinks it.
- **Repatriation compliance:** since late 2025 the bank needs SARS clearance before remitting proceeds, free to obtain but costly to start late, covered in the [repatriation guide](/guides/repatriating-property-sale-proceeds/).

Cape Town Invest treats these as calendar items, not budget items: almost every rand a non-resident seller loses here is timing, and timing is free to fix at mandate.

## How do you keep selling costs down?

Cost control on a Cape Town sale is mostly sequencing, and the achievable saving on a R4.5 million sale is R100,000 or more without cutting a single corner. Commission negotiation carries the largest number, and the timing items, notice, pre-inspection and early clearance, protect the rest of the stack for foreign buyers and locals alike.

The checklist Cape Town Invest runs at mandate:

- **Negotiate commission with structure,** not just a lower rate: a tiered mandate above target price routinely saves 0.5% to 1% of the price.
- **Give the bank its 90-day notice immediately,** eliminating penalty interest entirely.
- **Pre-inspect electrical, water, beetle, gas and fence** for under R2,000 before listing, converting remediation from a transfer delay into ordinary maintenance.
- **Pull the municipal statement and pay down arrears** before the clearance application.
- **For non-residents, lodge the NR03 directive and SARS clearance early,** turning tax timing from months into weeks.

None of these require leverage, only order: every item is executable in the first week of the mandate, before an offer exists.
