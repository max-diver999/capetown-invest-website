## What does it take to move sale proceeds out of South Africa?

Repatriating sale proceeds requires three components: proof of how the purchase money originally entered South Africa, a complete sale file from the conveyancer, and SARS clearance under rules that changed in late 2025. For foreign buyers who sell, banks remit within 1 to 6 weeks of registration, and a missing component can stretch the wait into months.

| Component | Created when | Provided by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inward transfer record | At purchase, when funds first arrived | Your authorised dealer bank |
| Sale file | During the 8 to 12 week transfer | Conveyancer |
| SARS clearance (AIT PIN or letter) | Applied for during the sale | SARS via eFiling or review |

The order of operations matters more than any single document. In our analysis of exit files, sellers who open the SARS thread the week the mandate is signed run the clearance in parallel with the [8 to 12 week transfer](/guides/property-transfer-timeline-delays/) and meet the money at registration. Sellers who start after registration add the full clearance time to the wait, with the proceeds sitting in rand throughout. The [full selling guide](/guides/selling-property-south-africa-non-resident/) shows where this thread sits among the other three tracks of a non-resident sale.

## Why does the bank need SARS clearance since late 2025?

SARS clearance became the bank's gatekeeper in late 2025: an authorised dealer now requires tax verification before remitting non-resident funds. Residents already needed an Approval for International Transfer above the R1 million discretionary allowance; the update pulls foreign buyers who sell into the same net, via an AIT PIN or a Manual Letter of Compliance.

The two routes serve two different sellers:

- **AIT PIN via eFiling:** for sellers registered with SARS, which includes anyone who filed returns on rental income. Apply under Tax Compliance Status, declare the property sale as the source, receive a PIN the bank validates directly with SARS.
- **Manual Letter of Compliance:** for sellers who never held a South African tax number. SARS reviews the sale file itself and issues a letter confirming no claim on the proceeds.

Cape Town Invest reads the practical effect simply: tax compliance is now a payment precondition, not an annual formality. A worked example from our analysis of exit files shows the stakes. A seller with two unfiled rental returns discovers the gap only when the AIT application bounces; filing, assessment and penalty settlement consume 6 to 10 weeks while R4 million in proceeds sits in the conveyancer's trust account earning trust interest instead of reaching the seller's mortgage offshore. The same seller, had the returns been filed in the ordinary cycle, would have held a PIN before registration. The rule did not change what tax was owed, it changed when the reckoning happens: at the exact moment the money wants to move. Sellers with [rental income histories](/guides/non-resident-rental-income-tax-south-africa/) should audit their SARS standing the day they decide to sell.

Neither route costs anything to apply for; both cost dearly to start late.

## Which documents make up the repatriation file?

The repatriation file is six documents, and a seller holding all six rarely gets stuck at the bank. Foreign buyers create the most important one on the day they buy: the inward transfer confirmation showing the purchase funds arriving through an authorised dealer, which no one can issue retrospectively without archive work.

| Document | Purpose | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inward transfer confirmation | Proves the capital entered legitimately | Lost after a decade, bank archives take weeks |
| Sale agreement | Establishes the price | Rarely a problem |
| Conveyancer's final statement | Accounts for every rand of the price | Requested late |
| Proof of section 35A payment | Shows the 7.5% to 15% reached SARS | Confused with CGT filing |
| Identity and FICA documents | Bank compliance | Expired certified copies |
| AIT PIN or compliance letter | SARS clearance since late 2025 | Applied for after registration |

Cape Town Invest checklists all six at mandate, and two deserve their own reading: the [section 35A guide](/guides/section-35a-withholding-tax-explained/) covers the withholding proof, and the [exchange control guide](/guides/south-africa-exchange-control-property/) covers how the inward record is created at purchase and why it defines what can leave later.

## How long does repatriation take?

Repatriation timing ranges from days to months, and the file, not the bank, sets the pace. Our analysis of seller outcomes puts a complete file at 1 to 2 weeks from registration to offshore receipt, the ordinary spread at 1 to 6 weeks, and a reconstruction case at 2 to 6 months.

| Scenario | Realistic timing |
| --- | --- |
| Complete file, AIT PIN in hand before registration | 1 to 2 weeks |
| PIN applied for at registration, clean tax history | 2 to 6 weeks |
| Manual Letter of Compliance from a standing start | 4 to 12 weeks |
| Unfiled returns to fix first | Add 6 to 10 weeks |
| Inward record reconstruction from old archives | 2 to 6 months |

A worked example sets the benchmark for a well-run exit. A German seller of a R3.8 million Sea Point flat, registered with SARS through 5 years of filed rental returns, signs the mandate in week zero, applies for the AIT PIN in week one, and holds it by week three while the transfer is still in its documentation phase. Registration lands in week eleven, the conveyancer's final statement follows within 3 business days, the bank's compliance desk validates the PIN and the inward record in another 4 business days, and the proceeds convert and land in Frankfurt twelve weeks after mandate, 8 days after registration. Every step ran inside the transfer timeline that was happening anyway, which is the entire design principle: the clearance is only a delay when it starts late.

Two rules of thumb fall out of the table:

- **Start the clearance at mandate,** and the slowest SARS route still finishes inside an 8 to 12 week transfer.
- **Fix the tax history first,** because no application moves faster than the oldest unfiled return behind it.

## What if the funds came in years ago or the property was inherited?

Old purchases and inherited properties are where repatriation files break, because the inward record either predates the seller's memory or never existed in their name. Banks archive transfer confirmations for foreign buyers, but retrieving a SWIFT record from 10 years back is a written request that runs 3 to 8 weeks, and it anchors everything the bank will approve.

Three situations, three fixes:

1. **Old purchase, record lost:** ask the original authorised dealer for an archive retrieval early; the conveyancer's historic file and the deeds record of the original transfer support the application.
2. **Inherited property:** the estate file replaces the inward record; the executor's liquidation account and the deceased estate's SARS clearance document the source instead.
3. **Bought while resident, emigrated since:** the sale is governed by your changed status; the [returning and emigrating expat guide](/guides/returning-south-african-expat-property/) covers the tax residency side, and the ceased-residency date drives which SARS process applies.

Insider tip: the archive request is free to start and slow to finish, so lodge it the day you consider selling, even before a mandate. A retrieved record that turns out unnecessary costs nothing; a missing record discovered at registration costs 2 to 6 months at exactly the moment the rand exposure is largest.

## How do you protect the value while the money waits?

Currency exposure is the hidden cost of a slow repatriation for foreign buyers, because proceeds sit in rand from registration until the bank remits. On R4 million waiting 3 months, a 3% rand move is R120,000, roughly the entire agent-negotiation saving most sellers fight for, surrendered to timing nobody managed.

The tools are ordinary and underused:

- **Forward contracts:** an authorised dealer or FX specialist can fix today's rate for a settlement 1 to 3 months out, matching the expected clearance date.
- **Trust account instructions:** proceeds can be held in the conveyancer's trust account earning interest for your benefit until the clearance lands, rather than moving to an ordinary account.
- **Split remittance:** where part of the file is clean, some banks will remit the documented portion while the balance waits, worth asking rather than assuming.
- **The refund tail:** any section 35A excess returns months later through [SARS assessment](/guides/south-africa-capital-gains-tax-property/), and that second, smaller repatriation needs the same clearance file, so keep it intact after the main transfer.

Cape Town Invest treats the FX decision as part of the exit plan, not an afterthought: the seller who fixes a forward rate at mandate has converted a market risk into a known number, which is what the entire four-track sale process is for.

## Buyer scenarios for repatriating sale proceeds

| Seller profile | Clearance route | Decision framework |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Non-resident with SARS number | AIT PIN via eFiling | File returns before registration week |
| Never registered for tax | Manual Letter of Compliance | Start letter when sale agreement signs |
| Inherited property | Estate file replaces inward record | Executor documents source of funds |
| Old purchase, lost SWIFT | Archive retrieval | Request from authorised dealer at mandate |

Scenario one: proceeds sit in rand for eight weeks while SARS reviews a manual letter because section 35A proof was uploaded incomplete. A forward contract booked at mandate would have locked the rate; waiting cost more than the FX fee. Scenario two: seller splits remittance, sending documented capital first while gain waits on assessment. Not every bank offers split remittance, but asking early avoids treating it as impossible.

The decision framework for foreign sellers is to build the repatriation file in the same folder as the sale file from purchase day. Inward transfer confirmation, improvement invoices, annual returns and the original OTP copy live in one digital bundle. At sale, the conveyancer and bank receive a complete pack instead of a scavenger hunt across email accounts.

After the main remittance, keep the compliance file for the section 35A refund tail. SARS may release excess withholding months later, and that second transfer needs the same clearance mechanics. Sellers who discard paperwork after the first wire often rediscover the gap when a refund sits unclaimed.

Sellers who never registered for tax should open the Manual Letter of Compliance track when the sale agreement is signed, not when proceeds hit the trust account. SARS reviews the full sale file, including proof that section 35A reached them within 21 days of registration. A UK seller who waits until registration to ask for the letter routinely adds four to eight weeks of rand exposure on R3 million or more.

If your purchase funds entered through a UK high-street bank rather than a South African authorised dealer, the inward record may not match what the bank expects today. Archive retrieval from the original dealer, or a reconstructive affidavit supported by old statements, is slower but still workable if started at mandate. The decision framework is chronological: prove source of funds first, tax clearance second, FX third.

Banks sometimes ask for a conveyancer letter confirming the sale price matches the trust account disbursement before releasing FX. Align your attorney, tax adviser and forex desk on one timeline email each Friday during clearance week so nobody waits on a document another party already holds.

AIT PIN applications through eFiling fail when outstanding provisional tax or old VAT registrations sit on a dormant number. Clear dormant registrations before you list, not after you have a buyer waiting on proceeds.
