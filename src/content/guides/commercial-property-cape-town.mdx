## What is actually different from a residential purchase?

Five things, and each of them changes a number rather than a formality. A residential buyer negotiates a price and pays transfer duty; a commercial buyer negotiates a price, a tax treatment and a lease at the same time.

| | Residential | Commercial |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax on transfer | Transfer duty above R1,210,000, up to 13% | VAT at 15%, potentially zero-rated to 0% |
| What sets value | Comparable sales and location | The lease, its term and the tenant's credit |
| Rates category | Residential, with R620,000 relief | Higher category, no residential relief |
| Financing basis | The buyer's income | The building's income |
| Main diligence document | Body corporate financials or title deed | The lease and its schedules |

The 15% figure is the one to hold. On a R10 million tenanted building, VAT at the standard rate is R1.5 million and the same building zero-rated as a going concern is nil, while a non-vendor seller at that price would attract transfer duty of roughly R937,000 on the progressive scale. Three treatments, three very different cheques on the same day.

The consequence is that a commercial deal takes longer to price. A residential buyer can form a view on a Saturday viewing; a commercial buyer cannot form one until they have read the lease, because the same building with a different tenant is a different asset.

## How does the going concern rule work?

It turns a 15% cash line into nothing, and it is the single biggest structural advantage in a tenanted commercial purchase. Where an income-producing property is sold as a going concern between two VAT-registered parties, the supply can be zero-rated for VAT, meaning no VAT is payable at transfer.

The conditions are specific. Both parties must be registered VAT vendors, the enterprise must be capable of separate operation, the sale must include what is needed to carry it on, meaning the leases, and the agreement must record in writing that the parties intend a going concern at the zero rate. A buyer cannot arrange this alone: if the seller is not a vendor, the route is unavailable and transfer duty applies instead.

Insider tip: get the VAT position confirmed in the offer rather than in the conveyancing. A deal priced on the assumption of zero-rating that turns out not to qualify leaves a buyer needing 15% more cash on a date that has already been set, and that is a financing problem rather than a negotiating one.

## Why is the lease the asset?

Because the income is what you are buying, and the lease is the contract that produces it. Two identical shells on the same street with different leases are different investments, and the difference is not marginal.

Six terms carry most of the value:

1. **Unexpired term.** A lease with eight years left and one with eight months left are not comparable, whatever the current rent.
2. **Escalation rate and its base.** An annual escalation compounds, and whether it runs off the base rent or off the previous year's rent changes the number materially over a decade.
3. **Who carries rates, insurance and maintenance.** A rent that looks strong before the landlord discovers they carry the rates is not strong.
4. **Deposit or bank guarantee held.** What secures the lease if the tenant fails.
5. **Renewal and break options.** Held by which party, on what notice, at what rent.
6. **The tenant's covenant.** The credit standing behind the promise, which is what actually pays the rent.

A tenanted building should be underwritten on the lease first and the property second. The building matters when the lease ends, which is why the unexpired term and the alternative use both belong in the model.

## What do commercial rates cost?

More than residential, on two counts. The City of Cape Town rates property by category and applies a higher cent-in-the-rand to commercial and industrial use than to residential, and the R620,000 rates-free portion that reduces a home's bill does not apply.

That makes the rates line a real operating cost rather than a rounding item, and it makes the property's rate category something to confirm rather than assume. Two situations catch buyers. A property whose use is changing, for example a house being converted to offices, moves category and the bill moves with it. And a property whose category on the valuation roll does not match its actual use is a correction waiting to happen, in whichever direction.

For scale, the residential tariff runs at roughly 0.64 cents in the rand with the first R620,000 of value exempt, so a R10 million residential property pays about R64,000 a year. A commercial property of the same value pays more, on both counts: a higher rate and no exemption. Confirm the current category and the applicable tariff with the City for the specific property before you model the net income, and read it against the [suburb price table](/guides/cape-town-property-prices-by-suburb-2026/) if you are converting a residential building. The [rates and taxes guide](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) sets out how the residential arithmetic works and where the relief applies, which is the baseline this departs from.

## What does diligence look like on a commercial building?

The residential checklist plus a set of questions that only arise when a building earns money. The [due diligence guide](/guides/due-diligence-cape-town-property/) covers the shared items, including the title deed search and the zoning position; the additions below are what a tenanted purchase needs on top.

| Document | What it tells you | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The lease and all addenda | The actual terms, not the summary | Summaries omit the break clause |
| Rent roll and arrears history | Whether the tenant pays on time | A strong lease badly performed is a weak asset |
| Municipal accounts, 12 months | Real rates, water and refuse | The seller's estimate is not the bill |
| Zoning certificate and approved plans | What the building may lawfully be used for | Unapproved work becomes your liability |
| Occupancy certificate | That the building may lawfully be occupied | Its absence is a serious problem |
| Fire and health compliance | Ongoing statutory obligations | These transfer with the building |

Insider tip: ask for twelve months of municipal accounts rather than a rates figure. The rates line is one part of a municipal bill that also carries water, sewerage and refuse on commercial tariffs, and on a building with a large footprint those lines are not small.

## Does this suit a foreign buyer?

Legally, yes, with no surcharge and no visa requirement, on the same basis as residential. Practically, commercial is harder for a non-resident than residential for one reason: the financing is assessed on the building's income rather than on the buyer's salary, and a bank lending against a lease wants to see the lease, the tenant and the covenant before it looks at the borrower.

Two other points apply as they do on any purchase. Rental income from a South African commercial property is South African income and the non-resident owner registers with SARS and pays tax on the local profit, which the [non-resident rental income guide](/guides/non-resident-rental-income-tax-south-africa/) sets out. And purchase funds should be introduced through an authorised dealer bank with the title deed endorsed non-resident, which protects repatriation of the proceeds on exit.

Commercial is a smaller and more specialised market than the residential stock this site mostly covers, and it rewards a buyer who reads leases. For the residential picture, start with the [metro investment guide](/guides/cape-town-property-investment-guide/).
