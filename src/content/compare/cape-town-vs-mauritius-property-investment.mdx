## Cape Town vs Mauritius: The Core Trade-Off

Cape Town and Mauritius answer different questions. Mauritius is scheme-gated and residency-linked, where non-citizens buy only approved PDS, RES, IRS or Smart City stock at thresholds often above USD 375,000. Cape Town is open market with no foreign surcharge, modelled coastal yields of 6 to 9%, and no visa attached to the deed.

Four differences carry most of the decision:

- Where you may buy: any Cape Town suburb, against a published scheme list in Mauritius.
- What the purchase buys you: a modelled 6 to 9% gross in Cape Town income nodes, or a residence permit route in a qualifying Mauritian scheme.
- Which currency you carry: rand rent with full swing, against dollar-quoted scheme tickets settled with rupee costs.
- Who buys it next: semigration and local upgraders, against a niche international scheme resale pool.

Mauritius channels non-citizen capital into IRS, RES, PDS, and Smart City envelopes with minimum investments that double as residency qualifiers, delivering island tourism liquidity and a managed currency, but capping where you can shop and often compressing yields into a 4 to 6% gross band once levies and seasonality are honest.

Cape Town lets foreigners buy broadly on the same transfer duty scale as locals, models stronger income in nodes such as Sea Point, and sits inside a Western Cape market up about 179.6% from 2010 to September 2025, powered by semigration rather than resort arrivals alone, but offers no property-linked visa and exposes you to rand moves. One market trades scheme gates for residency; the other trades currency volatility for open-market access and deeper domestic demand.

## Tax and Acquisition: Open Cape Town vs Scheme-Gated Mauritius

South Africa imposes no foreign buyer surcharge, so a foreigner buying in Cape Town pays the same transfer duty scale a local is charged: nothing on the first R1,210,000, rising to 13% on the top slice. Mauritius adds no nationality penalty either, yet non-citizens may only buy inside schemes approved by the Economic Development Board.

Those schemes are Integrated Resort Scheme legacy projects, Real Estate Scheme legacy stock, active Property Development Scheme launches, and qualifying Smart City developments. The boundary effectively sets a minimum ticket and a due diligence path unlike Cape Town's open market, where the same duty scale applies on freehold or sectional title across the Atlantic Seaboard, City Bowl, or Winelands.

| Acquisition factor | Cape Town | Mauritius |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Foreign buyer surcharge | None | None on nationality |
| Where foreigners may buy | Open market citywide | IRS, RES, PDS, Smart City only |
| Typical minimum entry | Median near R1.9m, area-dependent | Scheme thresholds, often USD 375,000+ for PDS residency track |
| Main transfer taxes | Transfer duty on national scale | Registration duty plus scheme levies |
| Annual wealth tax on home | None on residential ownership | None, but scheme service charges apply |
| Residence permit link | None | Qualifying scheme purchase can support permit |

Cape Town minimises friction at the point of entry and maximises choice of suburb and building. Mauritius concentrates foreign demand into regulated envelopes where developers pre-clear eligibility, which simplifies compliance but removes the option to pick up a random Sea Point equivalent outside the scheme list. For a buyer who already intended a USD 400,000 island lifestyle base with residency, Mauritius scheme stock is feature, not bug. For a buyer who wants to hunt yield in an established urban node at R3m to R8m without a developer gate, Cape Town is the cleaner fit. Verify current PDS minimums and registration duty with Mauritian professionals before you treat any threshold as fixed.

## Currency: Volatile rand vs Managed Mauritian Rupee

Currency shapes both entry price and repatriation story. The South African rand is volatile relative to the US dollar, euro, and pound, which makes Cape Town entry cheaper when the rand is weak and amplifies hard-currency gains if the rand recovers from stressed levels, but erodes the value of rand rent and capital when it slides further. Foreign buyers must record incoming funds through an authorised dealer bank with non-resident endorsement for clean repatriation, a process covered in [Buying Cape Town Property as a Foreigner](/guides/buy-cape-town-property-foreigner/).

The Mauritian rupee is managed and generally less volatile than the rand, offering relative calm for local operating costs. Scheme marketing, however, often prices units in US dollars or euros, which partially hard-currencies the investment even while utilities and staff run in MUR. That hybrid can suit buyers who want residency and a dollar-denominated ticket without full emerging-market currency swing, but it removes the deep discount a weak rand gives in Cape Town today. Cape Town is the higher-risk, higher-optionality currency play; Mauritius is moderate stability with scheme-level hard currency quoting.

Financing choice is where the rand actually hits the model. A non-resident borrowing locally is quoted off the South African prime rate, which sits near 10.5%, and banks typically cap lending to non-residents at about 50% of the purchase price, so half the ticket has to arrive as offshore capital in any case. Borrowing in rand hedges the asset against the currency it earns rent in, since the bond instalment and the rent move together, while funding the whole purchase from euros or pounds leaves you fully exposed to the rand on both rent and resale. Every inward transfer must be recorded through an authorised dealer bank with a non-resident endorsement, because that record is what allows the original capital and the gain to leave again. Decide the split between rand debt and offshore equity before you sign, not at transfer.

| Currency factor | Cape Town (ZAR) | Mauritius (MUR) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Volatility vs USD | High | Lower, managed |
| Effect on entry | Weak rand cuts hard-currency price | Scheme prices often USD-linked |
| Rental income FX | Rand converts with full swing | Mixed USD rents, MUR costs |
| Repatriation | Exchange control with recording | Scheme and bank rules apply |
| Upside case | Rand recovery amplifies returns | Stability, less rebound option |

## Residency: Mauritius Property Link vs Cape Town Clean Investment

Residency is the decisive non-financial fork. Mauritius grants a residence permit pathway when a non-citizen buys qualifying scheme property at or above Economic Development Board thresholds, often USD 375,000 or more on the PDS track. South Africa has never tied residency to property, and a Cape Town deed confers no visa.

Eligible Mauritian routes run through approved PDS developments, legacy RES or IRS projects, and Smart City schemes with their own minimums, subject to compliance, renewals, and professional advice. The property is not automatic citizenship, but it is a genuine immigration anchor.

Buying in Cape Town confers no visa, work right, or permanent residence shortcut. Non-residents enter through standard visa categories unrelated to the deed. If your goal includes a renewable island base with tax and lifestyle planning, Mauritius scheme property belongs in the conversation with an immigration lawyer. If residency is irrelevant and you want pure investment plus semigration exposure, Cape Town's clean separation keeps the asset decision honest.

| Residency factor | Cape Town | Mauritius |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Property grants residency | No | Yes, in qualifying schemes |
| Main immigration route | Standard visa categories | PDS, RES, IRS, Smart City thresholds |
| Link to purchase | None | Direct when conditions met |
| Best for residency goal | No | Yes, with scheme budget |

## Liquidity: Semigration Depth vs Island Tourism Resale

Cape Town liquidity is semigration breadth: foreign buyers account for roughly 25% of premium Atlantic Seaboard value, behind R11.3bn of turnover in that corridor in 2025. Mauritius liquidity is real but narrower, concentrated in approved schemes that trade among international lifestyle, tax planning and residency buyers.

Established RES and PDS names carry active resale. Turnover can slow when the Economic Development Board adjusts minimums, when new supply launches nearby, or when global buyers pause island second-home purchases. Tourism supports short-term letting, not automatically long-term exit timing. Cape Town favours semigration breadth; Mauritius favours niche international scheme demand. | Foreign buyer share | ~25% of premium Seaboard value | Concentrated in approved schemes |
| Exit breadth | Citywide nodes plus Winelands | Scheme and developer specific |

## Pros and cons: side by side

| Market | Pros | Cons |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cape Town | No foreign surcharge; open-market choice; modeled 6 to 9% yields; +179.6% provincial growth; semigration liquidity | Rand volatility; rental income taxable; no residency link |
| Mauritius | Property-linked residence permit in schemes; island tourism; managed MUR; hard-currency scheme pricing | Scheme-only purchase path; yields often 4 to 6% gross; seasonality; minimum investment gates |

Cape Town suits buyers who want urban coastal income with provincial growth tailwinds and freedom to choose any suburb. Mauritius suits buyers who budget for scheme minimums and value a residence permit anchor alongside island lifestyle, accepting lower modeled yield and corridor-specific resale.

Insider tip: a Cape Town exit ties up more cash up front than a Mauritius one. On any sale above R2,000,000 by a non-resident seller, section 35A obliges the conveyancer to withhold 7.5% of the price for an individual, 10% for a company, and 15% for a trust, and pay it to SARS at transfer. That money is credited against your final capital gains assessment, but it sits with SARS until the assessment is issued, so plan any onward purchase around the refund date rather than the transfer date.

## Scheme Residency vs Open-Market Yield

Mauritius is the market for island lifestyle, scheme governance and a property-linked residence permit, accepting IRS, RES, PDS or Smart City gates and gross yields modelled nearer 4 to 6%. Cape Town is the open-market answer: no foreign surcharge, modelled coastal income of 6 to 9%, inside a province up about 179.6% since 2010.

Neither route is a partial version of the other. Mauritius buyers accept tourism seasonality and a scheme gate in exchange for the permit; Cape Town buyers accept rand volatility and no visa from the deed in exchange for open-market choice and semigration depth to September 2025.

Neither market is universally superior. Residency-motivated buyers with scheme budgets should weigh Mauritius with immigration and tax counsel. Yield and flexibility buyers should weigh Cape Town with refreshed MODELED math in the [Cape Town Rental Yield Guide](/guides/cape-town-rental-yield-guide/). Decide whether you are buying a regulated island base or an open-market semigration asset first, then shortlist accordingly.
