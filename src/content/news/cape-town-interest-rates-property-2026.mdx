The South African Reserve Bank ended a long easing pause in May 2026. Governor Lesetja Kganyago and the Monetary Policy Committee voted for a 25 basis point increase, taking the policy repo rate to 7.00% and pushing the commercial prime lending rate to 10.50%. For Cape Town property buyers, the move is less about a credit crunch than about precision: every extra basis point changes maximum loan size, monthly cash flow, and the price band that still clears a bank stress test.

Cape Town remains one of the country's most liquid residential markets, but the Atlantic Seaboard and City Bowl price floors mean even a modest rate move filters out marginal applicants. Investors comparing cash and geared returns should pair this rate read with our [Cape Town property investment guide](/guides/cape-town-property-investment-guide/) and the full purchase sequence in [how to buy property in Cape Town](/guides/how-to-buy-property-cape-town-step-by-step/).

## What changed at the May 2026 MPC meeting

SARB last hiked in May 2023, then held or cut through a disinflation cycle. The May 2026 decision signals that the committee sees upside risk to inflation or rand stability strong enough to absorb a small tightening step. Banks priced the move within days: prime moved from 10.25% to 10.50%, while fixed-rate quotes for 20-year terms drifted higher on new applications.

| Rate benchmark | Level after May 2026 | Prior level | Buyer note |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Repo rate | 7.00% | 6.75% | Policy anchor for banks |
| Prime lending rate | 10.50% | 10.25% | Variable home loan base |
| 2024 cycle peak (prime) | 11.75% | n/a | Current tier still 125bp below peak |
| Typical fixed 20-year quote | 11.00% to 11.40% | Varies by bank | Shop multiple lenders |

The hike does not reverse the relief buyers gained since the 2024 peak, but it ends the narrative that rates would only fall. Anyone who underwrote a Cape Town purchase in early 2026 on the assumption of another 50bp cut should refresh numbers before lifting an Offer to Purchase.

## What does BetterBond data show for Cape Town bond demand?

BetterBond recorded bond application growth of about 6.2% year on year through early 2026, with Cape Town and the wider Western Cape among the stronger corridors even after the May hike lifted prime to 10.50%. Demand skews toward sectional title on the Atlantic Seaboard and in the City Bowl, while first-time buyers in the Northern Suburbs hit instalment ceilings first, because their applications sit closest to the debt-to-income cap banks apply near 30% of gross income. That growth sits alongside tighter bank scorecards. Lenders are approving deals, but with lower loan-to-value on non-primary homes and closer scrutiny of short-term rental income declared on bond applications. Non-residents meet a harder version of the same test: South African banks generally lend up to about 50% of value to a foreign buyer, so the balance arrives from offshore and must be recorded for clean repatriation later.

Foreign buyers should read [non-resident mortgage rules in Cape Town](/guides/non-resident-mortgage-cape-town/) before assuming offshore income will count at full value.

## Affordability maths for Cape Town buyers

On a R3,000,000 loan over 20 years at prime 10.25%, the instalment was near R29,400 per month. At 10.50%, it moves to roughly R29,900. Over a 20-year term that is close to R120,000 in extra interest, before fees. Buyers stretching to the top of their approval should negotiate a longer bond term, a larger deposit, or a lower purchase price.

| Purchase price | 10% deposit | Bond at 90% LTV | Instalment at 10.50% (20 yr) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| R2,500,000 | R250,000 | R2,250,000 | R22,400 |
| R3,500,000 | R350,000 | R3,150,000 | R31,400 |
| R5,000,000 | R500,000 | R4,500,000 | R44,800 |

Use the [cost of buying property in Cape Town](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-cape-town/) guide to stack transfer duty, bond registration, and conveyancing on top of instalments. A buyer approved for R3,150,000 still needs cash for duty and fees, often R150,000 to R200,000 on a R3,500,000 home.

## Stress-test your bond before you offer

A stress test is not a formality at prime 10.50%. The same R3,000,000 bond that cost about R29,400 a month at 10.25% now costs roughly R29,900, and another 100bp would push it higher again. Model the payment you would have to make in a worse cycle rather than the one your bank quotes today, then run the four steps below before you sign anything.

1. Model instalments at prime 10.50%, then again at 11.50% and 12.00%.
2. Keep total debt repayments under 28% of gross income at the stressed rate, not the quoted rate.
3. Hold six months of instalments in cash after transfer for rates, levies, and maintenance.
4. If you buy off-plan, confirm whether the developer payment plan ignores rate moves until occupation.

Off-plan purchases add timing risk. A unit delivering in 18 to 24 months may face a different prime at bond registration than at reservation. Read the [off-plan property guide for Cape Town](/guides/off-plan-property-cape-town-guide/) for deposit schedules and occupation-date clauses before you commit.

## Who wins and who pauses in Cape Town

Investors should separate yield from leverage before deciding whether the May 2026 move changes the plan at all. A Sea Point flat modelling 7% net yield can still run cash-positive at 10.50% prime if the deposit is 40% or higher, because the bond services only 60% of the price while the rent services the whole asset. A low-deposit play in a new development with delayed rental income can fail the same stress test even when the gross yield looks attractive on paper, since the instalment starts at bond registration and the rent starts at occupation. Cash buyers are the quiet winners of a 25bp move: it costs them nothing and thins the bidding at the margin. The buyers who should pause are those approved at 10.25% who have not re-run the numbers at 11.50%.

## What to do before your next viewing

SARB's next MPC decisions will track inflation prints and rand moves. Even if prime stays near 10.50% for several months, underwriting at the 2024 peak of 11.75% remains the conservative baseline for long-hold investors.
