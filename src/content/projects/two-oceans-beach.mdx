## Beiramar: fourteen units on Coral Road

Fourteen units is the defining fact, and it cuts both ways. A boutique scheme on Coral Road avoids the anonymity of a large block and gives owners a realistic chance of knowing how the building is run, but it also concentrates cost. Every communal expense, from lift servicing to the inverter installation to coastal-exposure painting cycles, divides across fourteen participation quotas rather than a hundred. Ask for the first-year body corporate budget and check how the sinking fund is being built, because a small scheme has very little room to absorb an unbudgeted repair without a special levy.

The product itself is straightforward: two and three-bedroom apartments from around R2.9 million to R2.98 million, generous balconies, lift access, and inverter backup for load-shedding continuity. Balcony area matters more here than in most Cape Town submarkets, since the West Coast southeaster can make an exposed terrace unusable for weeks in summer. Look at orientation and whether the balcony has any wind protection before you treat it as usable living space in a rental listing.

Location puts it near Infinity Blouberg Beach and within reach of Eden on the Bay retail, in the same corridor as the completed Infinity Apartments resale stock at 89 Coral Road. That resale complex is the most useful comparable you have: it shows what beachfront Coral Road actually lets for and what its levies run at, on a building that has been operating rather than one that has been drawn. Pull leases and levy statements from there before accepting any projection for a new scheme, and read the [Blouberg area guide](/areas/blouberg-property-investment/) for the wider rent bands.

## Transfer duty and VAT on off-plan

South African property transactions attract either VAT or transfer duty, never both. When you buy a primary unit directly from a VAT-registered developer, the price quoted is VAT inclusive and no transfer duty is payable. When you buy resale from a private seller, VAT does not apply and transfer duty does, on the SARS sliding scale that exempts the first R1,210,000 and then steps through 3%, 6%, 8%, 11%, and 13% bands. There is no foreign buyer loading on either side of that split.

The practical effect on a Blouberg beachfront ticket is real cash. A 2 On Hill unit at R2.695 million VAT inclusive carries no duty line at all. The same money spent on Coral Road resale would carry duty calculated across those bands, plus the usual conveyancing and deeds office costs, all payable up front and none of it recoverable in a resale price. That is a genuine advantage of primary off-plan stock and one of the few places where the off-plan route is cheaper rather than just later.

Three things to confirm in writing before you rely on it. First, that the seller is genuinely VAT registered and the agreement states the price is VAT inclusive: a sale from a private owner in a new building is resale, not primary, whatever the marketing implies. Second, what happens if the developer's VAT status or the pricing changes between reservation and transfer. Third, that bond registration costs, which are separate from duty, are budgeted. Work the totals through the [cost of buying guide](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-cape-town/) and have a conveyancer confirm the structure before deposit.

## Seasonal cash-flow model for short-stay

Beachfront short-stay income on the West Coast is not a monthly average, it is a curve. Summer fills the calendar at high nightly rates, shoulder months fill unevenly, and winter can go quiet for weeks at a time on a coastline that is exposed and windy out of season. A projection built by multiplying a January rate across twelve months will overstate annual revenue by a wide margin. Build the model month by month, assign a separate rate and occupancy assumption to each, and then sum.

The cost side does not follow the same curve. Levies, municipal rates, insurance, and any bond instalment are flat and due in the quiet months as well as the busy ones. Cleaning, linen, and platform commission scale with bookings, and the management fee sits at 20% to 25% of gross if you use an operator rather than running the listing yourself. New builds with backup power carry levy inflation that the file's own working assumption puts near 6% a year, so year three costs more than year one on the same revenue base.

Two disciplines keep this honest. Run the deal on a long-let base case first: the [Blouberg area guide](/areas/blouberg-property-investment/) models around 6.5% gross and 4.5% net on family stock, and underwriting on eleven months of rent in year one leaves room for a void. Then treat short-stay uplift as optional upside rather than the plan. Before any of it, confirm the body corporate conduct rules actually permit short-letting and check whether a night cap applies, because a scheme that prohibits it turns your whole seasonal model into a long-let by default.
