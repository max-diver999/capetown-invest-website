## Why does ONEHUNDREDONM heritage mixed-use positioning matter for investors?

Heritage adaptive reuse behind a retained Victorian streetscape changes risk and story at once. Approvals, structural surprises and phased demolition sit between deposit and occupation certificate, which is why the 7-storey rebuild was still on site through 2025 while Blok's completed stock at 343 Main Road was already letting at 4.5% to 6% gross.

Working around an existing structure is slower and less predictable than a clear-site build, and that unpredictability is priced into your carrying costs rather than the asking price.

On the story side, scarcity is the argument. Main Road frontage in Sea Point cannot be replicated, and a façade the City has an interest in preserving caps how many competing buildings can appear on the same block. Ground-floor retail and eateries feed the walkability that Fluent sells to guests and that long-let tenants pay a premium for, so the mixed-use element is income support for the residential floors rather than a separate profit centre for owners.

For underwriting, treat the heritage angle as a resale narrative rather than a yield input. It does not raise rent per square metre on its own. What it plausibly does is hold pricing better in a soft market, because buyers of design-led Atlantic Seaboard stock are usually motivated by location and product, not by a yield percentage. Check the retail lease structure and whether commercial tenants share the levy pool before you assume the arcade is a net positive on your monthly costs.

## Long-let versus serviced short-stay

Long-let is the base case at ONEHUNDREDONM and serviced short-stay is the option layered on top, not the reverse. A 12-month lease models at roughly 4.5% to 6% modelled gross, compressing to about 3.5% to 5% net once levies of R2,800 to R4,500 a month, municipal rates, and letting commission come out. Fluent serviced stays can beat that in peak months, but the management share runs near 15 to 25 percent of revenue, cleaning and linen recur per stay, and wear on a studio turned over weekly is not theoretical. Low-season occupancy is where the two strategies separate, so ask for month-by-month figures from 343 Main Road rather than an annual average. City short-term rental compliance and the body corporate conduct rules decide whether the option exists at all.

| Strategy | Gross income potential | Cost and risk | Best fit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Long-let 12-month lease | modelled 4.5% to 6% gross | Lower operating intensity | Absentee foreign owner base case |
| Fluent serviced stays | Can exceed long-let in peak months | Management share, cleaning, wear | Hands-off owner accepting fee drag |
| Owner-use with occasional let | Non-yield focus | Body corporate rules | Lifestyle buyer |

Underwrite long-let first. If serviced stays remain attractive, treat peak-season upside as optional, not base case, and confirm City of Cape Town short-term rental compliance.
