## What is left

Eleven homes, and the mix tells you something.

| Remaining | Type |
| ---: | --- |
| 9 | Double-storey homes |
| 2 | Single-level homes |

Phase one sold out and phase two is over 80% sold, with 114 units recorded as sold across the estate. A final release is a different shopping experience from a launch: the choice is narrow, the negotiating position is different on both sides, and the homes still standing are the ones earlier buyers walked past.

That is not a criticism of the stock. Double-storey homes sell more slowly than single-level ones in any estate with an older buyer profile, which is exactly what a lock-up-and-go Winelands estate has, so nine of eleven being double-storey is a predictable outcome rather than a warning.

Insider tip: on a final release, ask for the price list on the specific remaining homes rather than working from the published starting figure. The entry price usually belonged to a unit type that cleared in phase one.

## What lock-up-and-go actually signals

The buyer profile, and therefore the resale market.

Rabie positions Le Boutonne as lock-up-and-go: homes meant to be left for weeks at a time without maintenance accumulating, in a secure estate with green open space and everyday convenience close by. That phrase does more work than it looks like it does. It identifies a buyer who is not there every week, which typically means semigration families splitting time, retirees, and second-home owners.

Three consequences follow for anyone buying as an investment rather than a home:

1. **The letting market is thinner than the metro.** Fewer tenants want a Winelands estate than want a Sea Point flat, and those who do sign longer leases.
2. **Resale is slower and less seasonal.** Winelands stock does not turn over on the short-let calendar, which cuts both the volatility and the liquidity.
3. **Security infrastructure is a running cost.** The estate levy carries it, alongside municipal rates, which the [security estates guide](/guides/security-estates-cape-town-foreign-buyers/) covers.

## The numbers a buyer should have straight

Two taxes, one lending cap, and a levy.

Buying from a VAT-registered developer the figure a developer quotes has 15% VAT built into it and carries no duty, which is usually the cheaper position. A later resale at R4.88m attracts duty on the SARS scale in the 11% band with the conveyancing stack restored, and the [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) works out what that comes to.

A non-resident meets a further limit, because a non-resident is usually held to about half the value on a local bond. At R4.88m that is roughly R2.4m in cash before costs, which is the number that decides whether this is available to a foreign buyer at all.

The estate levy is the recurring line and is separate from municipal rates, which are billed against the valuation roll rather than the price you paid and continue for as long as you own the home. The [rates and taxes guide](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) works through the municipal side, and the [suburb price table](/guides/cape-town-property-prices-by-suburb-2026/) places Paarl against the rest of the region.
