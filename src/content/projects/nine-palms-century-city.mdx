## How should you underwrite Nine Palms Century City?

Nine Palms matters because it was the demand signal that unlocked Rabie's Ratanga Park residential pipeline. Before [On Park](/projects/on-park-century-city/) and before the canal-facing [SkyWater](/projects/skywater-century-city/) launch, Nine Palms proved that buyers would absorb more than 130 units on the park edge at record pace. Rabie's own commentary notes record sales at launch and semigration-driven demand from across South Africa and the continent, which is exactly the rental depth foreign investors want to see before they buy sectional title far from the Atlantic Seaboard.

For an international buyer, Nine Palms is now a completed-stock play, not an off-plan story. That shifts the due diligence from brochure projections to audited body corporate accounts, actual levy trends, and live rental comparables on Property24 and through local letting agents. The scheme targeted EDGE certification from the Green Building Council of South Africa, which can support lower running costs and tenant appeal, but you should confirm the final certification status on the unit you target.

## Park-front positioning and rental demand

Tenant demand in Century City is driven by corporate professionals, Canal Walk and Century City office workers, and semigration families who want suburban security with city access. Nine Palms one-bedroom units around 50 square metres trade on portals in the high-R2-millions, which implies gross yields in the 7% band if monthly rent sits in the R14,000 to R17,000 range, but you must verify live asking rent for the exact stack and aspect before you model.

Rental demand in the precinct is easier to verify here than in most Cape Town schemes, because Nine Palms has been occupied since late 2023 and the body corporate now has real trading history. Model it from the bottom up. A one-bedroom of about 50 square metres bought at R2.75 million and let at R15,000 a month produces R180,000 of gross rent, or roughly 6.5% gross before a single cost comes off. Push the rent to R17,000 and the same unit models near 7.4%. Take off the Century City levy, municipal rates, letting management at 8% to 10% of collected rent and about a month of vacancy, and roughly 4.2% net is what remains. Every one of those inputs is checkable against the scheme's own accounts and current portal listings.

Long-let is the base case for foreign owners who cannot self-manage. Short-let is possible in some Century City blocks but adds regulation and body corporate rule risk, so underwrite long-let first.

## Resale economics: transfer duty versus off-plan VAT

A South African property transaction attracts either VAT or transfer duty, never both, and which one applies depends entirely on the seller. When Rabie sold these units off-plan in 2022, the developer was a VAT-registered seller, VAT sat inside the advertised price, and the buyer paid no transfer duty. Primary stock is closed, so every purchase here now comes from a private owner who is not VAT-registered, which flips you onto the transfer duty side of the rule.

That flip is a real cash cost and it is the most common modelling error investors make when comparing a resale here against off-plan stock in the same precinct. The SARS table effective 1 April 2025 exempts the first R1,210,000 and then steps through 3%, 6%, 8%, 11%, and 13% on the portions above. On a R2.75 million resale that lands at R87,200 in duty, with conveyancing of roughly R28,000 to R32,000 on top, before any bond registration cost. None of it is financeable and all of it is payable in rand before registration. Set against the price, duty and conveyancing together run about 4.2% of a R2.75 million purchase, which at a 4.2% net yield is close to a full year of net rent. Treat that as part of the entry price rather than a closing formality.

| Line | Off-plan from developer | Resale from private seller |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax applied | VAT, inside the price | Transfer duty, on top |
| Extra cash at transfer | None for the tax itself | Duty plus conveyancing |
| What you underwrite on | Developer projections | Audited body corporate accounts |
| Handover risk | Build programme | None, occupied stock |

The trade is straightforward. Off-plan saves you the duty line but hands you completion risk and estimated levies. Resale costs you the duty but lets you verify actual rent, actual levies, and actual trustee minutes before you commit. For a foreign buyer underwriting from offshore, that verification is usually worth more than the tax saving. Run both through the [cost guide](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-cape-town/) and confirm the seller's VAT status in writing with your conveyancer before signature.

## What red flags should Nine Palms resale buyers treat as stop signals?

Stop when resale is priced like off-plan VAT without transfer duty and conveyancing in the worksheet, when gross yield uses peak summer short-let without long-let fallback, when body corporate financials are older than 12 months, when On Park and SkyWater supply is ignored in rent growth models, or when special levy risk is unread in trustee minutes.

- Resale priced like off-plan VAT without adding transfer duty and conveyancing to your worksheet.
- Gross yield quoted from peak summer short-let without a long-let fallback model.
- Body corporate financials older than 12 months on a scheme still settling owner mix.
- Ignoring new supply from On Park and SkyWater when underwriting rent growth.
- Special levy risk not reviewed in trustee minutes before offer.
