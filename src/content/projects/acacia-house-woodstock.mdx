## How should you underwrite Woodstock Albert Road?

Acacia House by Blok does not exist in Woodstock — [Blok](/developers/blok-urban-developers/) builds on the Atlantic Seaboard and De Waterkant, not Albert Road. Portfolio slug `acacia-house-woodstock` captures portal noise; the verified investable anchors are **WEX1** (85 Albert Road, Signatura + Indigo) and **The Iron Works** (Victoria / Ravenscraig, Land Equity), both completed around 2019 with mature resale and rental data.

Woodstock suits income investors who want City Bowl fringe tickets near R1.5m–R2.7m and MODELED net near 5%–6% after levies — not trophy capital preservation. Pair this review with the [Woodstock area guide](/areas/woodstock-property-investment/) and [Observatory pipeline](/projects/observatory-green/) before you double-count tenant overlap.

## The Iron Works: upper Woodstock resale

Land Equity Group's scheme sits on the corner of Victoria and Ravenscraig Roads, higher up the slope than the Albert Road strip, and that geography does most of the work in the resale case. Delivery landed around 2019 with roughly 136 one- and two-bedroom units between 50 and 106 square metres, launched at about R1.3 million to R2.8 million including VAT. Six years of tenancy history means you are underwriting actual rent rolls rather than a launch brochure, which is the single biggest advantage this block has over anything still on plan.

Marketed rentals from about R10,500 per month at launch give you a floor, not a current number. Ask the managing agent for the scheme's own rent roll spread, then check what has actually re-let in the last two quarters. Upper Woodstock stock in the 50 to 70 square metre band tends to clear faster than the larger 100 square metre plans, because the tenant pool here is single professionals and couples rather than families.

Three things drive the discount or premium on any individual unit: parking bay allocation on title, whether the unit faces the noisier Victoria Road frontage, and the special levy history. Pull three years of body corporate financials and check the reserve fund balance against the ten-year maintenance plan. A scheme of this vintage is entering its first serious external repaint and waterproofing cycle, and that lands as a levy increase or a special levy. Price it in before you offer.

Choosing between the two blocks is really a choice between amenity and levy discipline. Iron Works is residential-led, so the body corporate carries fewer shared plant items and the levy per square metre usually reads lower on comparable two-bedroom stock.

## Albert Road amenity and tenant demand

The Albert Road corridor is the reason Woodstock rents hold up without Atlantic Seaboard pricing. WEX1 carries a Spar and a Tops at ground level, plus pool and gym inside the scheme, so a tenant can cover groceries and fitness without leaving the block. The Old Biscuit Mill sits a short walk east and anchors the weekend footfall that keeps the design studios, coffee roasteries, and gallery tenancies along the strip commercially viable. That commercial density is what a letting agent actually sells when they show a unit.

Commute is the second lever. The MyCiTi trunk route runs the corridor, N2 access is immediate, and a cyclist reaches the CBD in about fifteen minutes. Tenants who work in the city centre or the Foreshore treat Woodstock as a discount on City Bowl rent rather than a compromise, which is why voids here track office attendance patterns more closely than they track tourism.

Be honest about the downside in your vacancy assumption. Albert Road is a working urban street with visible informality, uneven pavement quality, and street noise that does not read well on a Sunday viewing. The tenant who tolerates that is typically a creative-sector professional or a remote worker in their late twenties to late thirties, not a relocating family and not a corporate expat on an allowance. Underwrite one tenant profile, verify it against three live listings on the same stretch, and ignore the wider [City Bowl](/guides/cape-town-city-bowl-property-investment/) averages that blend in Gardens and Tamboerskloof stock.

## Transfer duty and VAT on resale

The rule that trips up most foreign buyers here is that a property transaction attracts either VAT or transfer duty, never both. When a VAT-registered developer sells a new unit, the price is VAT-inclusive and the buyer pays no transfer duty. The Iron Works launch pricing of about R1.3 million to R2.8 million including VAT is exactly that structure. When you buy from a private seller who is not VAT-registered, which is what almost every WEX1 and Iron Works resale is today, transfer duty applies instead and it is a real cash cost on top of the purchase price.

SARS bands effective 1 April 2025 put the first R1,210,000 at zero, then step up through 3%, 6%, 8%, 11%, and 13% on the portions above. There is no foreign buyer surcharge, so a Berlin or London purchaser pays the same as a Cape Town one. On the resale two-bedroom bands cited for these blocks, duty is a four-figure to low five-figure rand number, meaningful but not decisive.

| Sale type | What applies | Who pays cash for it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Developer primary sale, VAT-registered seller | VAT in the price | Nobody separately, it is embedded |
| Private resale, non-VAT seller | Transfer duty on SARS bands | Buyer, on top of price |
| Seller is a VAT-registered entity | VAT, no duty | Confirm in the offer wording |

Get your conveyancer to confirm the seller's VAT status in writing before signature, because an offer drafted as "plus VAT" versus "VAT inclusive" moves your total outlay materially. Full cost stack in the [buying cost guide](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-cape-town/) and the mechanics in [transfer duty explained](/guides/south-africa-transfer-duty-explained/).

## Rental agent and tenant vetting

If you are buying from offshore you are not managing this yourself, so the agent selection is part of the investment, not an afterthought. Long-let management in Cape Town typically runs 8% to 10% of collected rent, sometimes with a separate procurement fee of roughly one month on a new tenancy. Short-stay management sits nearer 20% and only matters if the body corporate conduct rules permit it, which in a mixed-use scheme with a hotel component is a question to answer before you buy rather than after.

Vetting is where the fee earns out. A competent agent runs a TPN or Experian credit check, verifies employment and three months of bank statements, confirms affordability against a rent-to-income ratio around a third, and calls the previous landlord rather than the reference the tenant supplied. Ask any prospective agent to show you their actual vetting pack and their arrears escalation timeline, not a description of it.

Two structural points for non-resident landlords. Eviction runs through the PIE Act and the courts, so a defaulting tenant is a months-long process regardless of what the lease says. One vacant month on a two-bedroom unit already removes roughly 8 percent of annual gross rent, and a contested eviction can cost several times that before the unit relets, which is why the 8 percent to 10 percent management fee is worth paying to an agent who screens properly. Build that into your void assumption, not just your rent assumption. And your rental income is South African-source and taxable here whether or not you are resident, so agree at appointment that the monthly statement will be clean enough for a tax return. Deposits must sit in an interest-bearing trust account with interest accruing to the tenant, and the move-in and move-out inspection reports determine whether you can claim against that deposit at all.

## NHBRC and mixed-use body corporate risk

Mixed-use precincts can carry higher reserve fund requirements when hotel lifts and pool plant serve multiple zones. Read the ten-year maintenance plan in body corporate minutes before you assume R3,000 levies stay flat.

Signatura's WEX Living award press coverage helps resale storytelling — but awards do not guarantee levy discipline. Numbers in audited financials beat marketing.

Albert Road's MyCiTi trunk and N2 access matter for tenant retention: creatives who cycle to the CBD in fifteen minutes tolerate Woodstock grit; tenants who need Atlantic Seaboard commutes often choose [Foreshore Place](/projects/foreshore-place-cape-town/) instead. Underwrite vacancy against that commute trade-off, not only against headline asking rent on Property24.
