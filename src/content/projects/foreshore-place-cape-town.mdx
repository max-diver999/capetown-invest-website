## Mixed-use layout and tenant demand

Foreshore Place stacks 171 residential units above office levels, ground-floor retail, parking podiums and an international hotel brand, so the tenant base is wider than a pure residential block. Finance, legal and government-adjacent workers rent the one-bedrooms that resell at R2m to R3m, and the fourth-floor lounge carries co-working demand from downstairs.

## Resale economics and transfer duty

The tower is completed, so there is no developer primary stock left and every purchase here is a resale from a private seller. That has one immediate consequence for your cash budget: transfer duty applies, and it is payable in rand before registration, not financed into the bond. The SARS table effective 1 April 2025 exempts the first R1,210,000 and then steps through 3%, 6%, 8%, 11%, and 13% on the portions above. There is no surcharge for foreign buyers, so a non-resident pays exactly what a local pays.

On the launch band cited for this building, roughly R1.1 million to R4.2 million, a studio near the lower end can fall inside or just above the exemption threshold, while a two-bedroom in the upper band carries a materially larger duty bill. Add conveyancing fees, deeds office charges, and bond registration costs if you are borrowing. Budget the full stack rather than the duty line alone.

Resale value inside the building is not uniform. Floor level, aspect, whether the unit looks over the mall or into a neighbouring facade, and the parking bay allocation all move price per square metre. So does the levy: a high monthly levy on a studio eats the yield that made the studio attractive in the first place. Run that arithmetic on the actual statement, because at a modelled gross of 5% to 7% a studio bought near R1.1 million produces roughly R4,500 to R6,500 of rent a month, and a combined levy and rates line of R2,500 leaves a thin margin once an 8% to 10% management fee and a one-month annual vacancy come out. Municipal rates themselves apply only above the R450,000 exemption, at about 0.69 cents in the rand. Get the levy statement for the specific unit, not the scheme average. Mechanics in [transfer duty explained](/guides/south-africa-transfer-duty-explained/), full costs in the [cost guide](/guides/cost-of-buying-property-cape-town/).

## Who should buy Foreshore Place and who should choose Charlotte or Waterfront stock?

This building suits an investor who wants CBD income at the lowest realistic entry ticket and is content with a conventional long-let. The unit mix says so plainly: 63 studios and 99 one-bedrooms against nine two-bedrooms means the scheme was built for single professionals and couples, and your tenant pool is finance, legal, tourism, and government-adjacent workers who want to walk to work. If your hurdle is a modelled net in the 3.5% to 5.5% band with a large letting pool behind it, this is the right shape of asset.

Choose [The Charlotte](/projects/the-charlotte-cape-town/) instead if you want hospitality income with the operating burden handled for you. Thirty-five units under aparthotel management is a different product: the fee stack includes housekeeping and front desk, the revenue is nightly rather than monthly, and the developer-claimed returns need stress-testing against occupancy assumptions you did not set. It is an operating business you own a share of, not a lease you collect.

Choose Waterfront or Silo District stock if the objective is capital preservation and currency-strong resale liquidity rather than yield. Those tickets are multiples of a Foreshore studio and the modelled net band sits lower, but the buyer pool on exit is deeper and less price-sensitive.

The mistake to avoid is buying Foreshore Place expecting short-stay economics. The hotel component and body corporate rules can restrict nightly letting in the residential sections, and that restriction is the single assumption most likely to break an imported spreadsheet.

## What belongs on your insider checklist?

An insider checklist for Foreshore Place is five documents and one arithmetic test. Ask for the body corporate levy schedule, the hotel floor programme, written confirmation on short-let consent, a conveyancer's pro-forma including transfer duty above the R1,210,000 threshold, and two independent rental appraisals for the same bedroom count on the CBD corridor.

Ask the agent for the latest body corporate levy schedule, the hotel floor programme, and whether short-term letting requires body corporate consent. Confirm parking bay allocation and monthly parking tariffs if not included in levy. Request a written pro-forma transfer cost from a conveyancer including transfer duty on your offer price. Pull two independent rental appraisals for the same bedroom count on the Foreshore or City Bowl corridor. If gross yield still clears your hurdle after a one-month vacancy assumption, proceed; if not, walk to [Century City](/guides/century-city-property-investment-guide/) income stock.

## Location: Foreshore, st georges mall, and the cbd commute

The 34-storey tower stands at Adderley Street, Riebeek Street and St Georges Mall, the hinge between the Foreshore office district and the pedestrianised spine of the old CBD. For a tenant in a one-bedroom that resells at R2m to R3m, the commute is a walk and a car is optional, which is real money in a parking-scarce city.

Legal and financial employers cluster within a few blocks, the Civic Centre and government offices are close, and the mall carries the daytime foot traffic that keeps ground-floor retail trading.

The Foreshore side of the address carries the district's known weaknesses. It was built for cars and offices, the elevated freeway ramps dominate the streetscape, and after the offices empty the immediate blocks go quiet in a way that Sea Point or Gardens do not. Tenants who want evening street life gravitate to Bree, Loop, and Kloof rather than Riebeek Street. Weekend footfall on St Georges Mall is thinner than weekday.

That profile shapes who rents from you. The reliable tenant here is someone whose working life is inside a ten-minute walk and who values that over neighbourhood atmosphere. Underwrite against office attendance patterns in the CBD rather than tourism seasonality, price a 1 month vacancy into any R2m to R3m one-bedroom, and check the walking route from the building to the nearest MyCiTi stop and grocery yourself rather than trusting a map. Broader district context sits in the [City Bowl guide](/guides/cape-town-city-bowl-property-investment/).

## Studio versus two-bedroom stock at Foreshore Place

Studios dominate the unit mix with 63 doors versus only nine two-bedroom apartments, which shapes the investor universe. Studios attract young professionals and student-adjacent demand tied to the nearby Cape Station student accommodation precinct, supporting higher void-adjusted turnover but lower absolute rent per door. One-bedroom units are the workhorse at 99 apartments, balancing ticket size near R2m to R3m resale with broader tenant demand. Two-bedroom stock is scarce and may suit small families or executive sharers willing to pay premium per square metre for mall-adjacent space.

When you compare Foreshore studios to [The Charlotte](/projects/the-charlotte-cape-town/) studios, remember Charlotte's aparthotel management may include housekeeping and front desk in the fee stack, while Foreshore is conventional sectional title unless a unit participates in hotel pooling. Normalize net cash flow per square metre before you choose between them.

Foreign cash buyers should pre-clear FICA documents and rand liquidity for transfer duty before bidding, because Foreshore resales move quickly when listings are scarce and delayed FX transfers have cost buyers their deposit slots in other CBD towers. Two numbers drive that timing. Transfer duty is payable in rand before registration and cannot be rolled into the bond, so on any price above the R1,210,000 exemption the cash must already be in the country when the conveyancer calls for it. South African banks also lend non-residents only about 50% of value, which means the other half arrives from offshore through an authorised dealer bank and has to be recorded for later repatriation. Keep a conveyancer on standby before you make a verbal offer, and ask your bank how many working days an inbound transfer of that size genuinely takes.
