## What can be verified, and what cannot

Worth separating, because most of what a search returns about this building is inference.

| Verifiable | Not published |
| --- | --- |
| A Blok development, listed in the developer's own sitemap | Unit count |
| Located in Sea Point | Number of floors |
| Complete, with no developer stock | Launch pricing |
| The developer's page for it now redirects to the homepage | Completion date |

We could fill the right-hand column from a portal listing or an old marketing page and nobody would check. We have not, because a number nobody can trace is worse than an acknowledged gap, and because the developer withdrawing its own page is itself the most useful fact on this page.

Insider tip: when a developer's site has removed a scheme, the body corporate is the authoritative source for what the building actually contains. The managing agent can tell you the number of units, the levy and the arrears position in one call, and none of it depends on marketing material.

## The sitemap problem behind the empty search results

Blok advertises 21 development pages that all redirect to its homepage.

This is worth stating because it explains why a search for TENONQ, or for any of Blok's completed buildings, returns so little that is authoritative. The developer's sitemap still lists every scheme URL. Every one of those URLs now serves a redirect to the homepage rather than a page about the building.

For a search engine that means twenty-one advertised addresses with no content behind them. For a buyer it means the most obvious source, the people who built it, has nothing to say. Our pages on [ONEHUNDREDONM](/projects/onehundredonm-sea-point/), [343 on B](/projects/three43onb-sea-point/) and [NINEONS](/projects/nineons-green-point/) cover the three Blok buildings for which we hold verified detail from when those pages were live.

## What Blok builds, and why the size matters more than the style

Small buildings in urban precincts, and small is the operative word.

Blok describes itself as an urban precinct developer, working across seven neighbourhoods over twelve years, with 22 developments, 1,089 homes built and 1,084 sold, from a staff of 42. It operates from 82 Main Road in Sea Point, which is the same market it builds in. The portfolio names, ONEONR, SIXONN, FORTYONL, TENONV, pair a number with a street initial.

The design attracts the buyer and the size sets the risk. In a scheme of twenty or thirty units, a single owner in arrears is several percent of the levy roll, one deferred maintenance decision falls on very few people, and the resale pool at any moment may be one apartment or none. The [Blok developer page](/developers/blok-urban-developers/) works through exactly what a small scheme concentrates.

## Buying or renting in a completed building

Two routes, one of them irregular, and the same homework for both.

A resale is a private transaction with an existing owner. It carries transfer duty on the SARS scale plus conveyancing, where a developer sale would have carried 15% VAT inside the price and no duty, so the front-end cost is heavier than the original buyers met. At Sea Point levels that lands in the upper bands: a R3,000,000 flat attracts 11% on the slice above R2,994,800, and a R5,000,000 one considerably more. A non-resident meets a second limit, because local banks rarely fund past 50% of value, and a letting agent takes 8% to 10% of rent if you let rather than occupy. The [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) prices that at any figure. A rental depends on an owner listing, which in a small building is irregular.

Four documents decide whether a resale here is a good one:

1. **The last two AGM minutes**, which show what the owners have actually disagreed about.
2. **The levy history**, because a small scheme's levy moves more sharply than a large one's.
3. **The reserve fund position**, which decides whether the next roof arrives as a special levy.
4. **The arrears schedule**, which in a twenty-unit building is a concentration risk rather than a statistic.

The [body corporate due diligence guide](/guides/body-corporate-due-diligence-cape-town/) covers how to read all four, and the [levies guide](/guides/sectional-title-levies-cape-town/) covers what the numbers should look like.

## Advantages and disadvantages of a small Sea Point scheme

The scarcity that supports the price is the same thing that thins the market.

The advantages: a design-led building in the densest and most consistently demanded apartment market in Cape Town, few competing units when you come to sell, and an owner body small enough that decisions get made. The disadvantages: every shared cost lands on very few owners, a single arrears case is material, the resale pool is thin in both directions, and in this specific case the developer has published nothing about the building since it sold out.

Sea Point itself is the strongest part of the case. It is the deepest apartment market on the Atlantic Seaboard, drawing local buyers, tenants and foreign owners at once, and Blok has built into it for 12 years rather than arriving for one cycle. The [Sea Point page](/areas/sea-point-property-investment/) covers the local market and the [Atlantic Seaboard guide](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) places it in the corridor.
