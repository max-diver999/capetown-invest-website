## How should Cape Town Invest readers underwrite NINEONS?

Portfolio feeds sometimes list NINEONS as active off-plan Green Point stock. The verified product is a handed-over Blok building on Scholtz Road: 23 sectional title apartments with a communal rooftop pool deck overlooking Green Point Common. There is no primary sales office inventory today — diligence runs on resale deeds, levies, and body corporate health.

NINEONS sits in the yield-and-lifestyle band between [Sea Point](/areas/sea-point-property-investment/) income density and Camps Bay prestige pricing. Green Point models around 8.0% gross and 6.0% net on typical apartments; NINEONS adds Blok design premiums and rooftop amenity costs that compress net unless short-stay occupancy is strong and permitted.

Foreign retirees and semigration buyers often shortlist Green Point for V&A Waterfront walkability without Atlantic Seaboard beachfront tickets. ## Blok design and amenity stack?

Blok builds design-led boutique stock, and the specification here reflects that: a shared rooftop deck and pool looking over Green Point Common toward Three Anchor Bay, stormwater tanks, solar water heating, and low-energy fittings through the units. The sustainability items are not decoration. Water tanks and solar heating reduce the municipal exposure that hits Cape Town owners hardest in a dry year, and they are the kind of infrastructure that is expensive to retrofit into a 1970s walk-up on the same street.

The scale is the defining feature. Twenty-three apartments, split into eight one-bedrooms, thirteen two-bedrooms, and two duplex penthouses above 200 square metres, means a very small body corporate. That has advantages: decisions get made and the design does not degrade through inconsistent owner alterations. It also means the rooftop pool, the deck, the lift, the tanks, and the pump plant are maintained by 23 owners rather than 100, so the levy per door is structurally higher than a plain block with no amenity. The two penthouses carry a large share of the participation quota, which shapes voting and levy allocation.

Model this honestly rather than treating amenity as free upside. Ask for three years of levy statements, the reserve fund position against the ten-year maintenance plan, and any special levy history since occupation in 2018. A pool and solar system reaching their first major service cycle is a normal cost, not a red flag, but it needs to be funded from reserves rather than a surprise resolution. Design premium supports resale and tenant appeal; it does not lower the running cost of the building.

## Foreign buyer workflow on resale?

1. Offer on live listing with suspensive bond clause if financing locally.
2. FICA through conveyancer; funds via authorised dealer.
3. Transfer duty on resale (no VAT on secondary sales).
4. Non-resident endorsement on title for repatriation.
5. Bond up to about 50% LTV for qualifying non-residents.
