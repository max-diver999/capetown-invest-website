## Blok design and amenity stack

Blok's specification at NINEONS is design-led: a shared rooftop deck and pool over Green Point Common, stormwater tanks, solar water heating and low-energy fittings across 23 sections, two of them duplex penthouses above 200 m². Tanks and solar heating cut the municipal exposure that hits Cape Town owners hardest in a dry year.

The scale is the defining feature. Twenty-three apartments, split into eight one-bedrooms, thirteen two-bedrooms, and two duplex penthouses above 200 square metres, means a very small body corporate. That has advantages: decisions get made and the design does not degrade through inconsistent owner alterations.

Amenity cost is the other side of the trade. The rooftop pool, the deck, the lift, the tanks, and the pump plant are maintained by 23 owners rather than 100, so the levy per door is structurally higher than a plain block with no amenity. The two penthouses carry a large share of the participation quota, which shapes voting and levy allocation.

Model this honestly rather than treating amenity as free upside. Four documents settle it:

- 36 months of levy statements per section, not a single current-year figure.
- Reserve fund position measured against the maintenance plan for the next 10 years.
- Special levy history since occupation in 2018.
- First major service costings for the pool, lift and solar plant.

A pool and solar system reaching their first major service cycle is a normal cost, not a red flag, but it needs to be funded from reserves rather than a surprise resolution. Design premium supports resale and tenant appeal; it does not lower the running cost of the building.

## Rental and short-stay economics (modelled)

Green Point models around 8.0% gross and 6.0% net on typical apartments, and NINEONS sits below that on net because 23 owners carry a rooftop pool, a lift and a solar plant between them. Whether short-stay letting is permitted swings the answer more than any other single variable. Price these four lines before you model a single night's rate:

- Body corporate levy per door (boutique concierge-style buildings often exceed plain towers).
- City of Cape Town municipal rates on your unit share.
- Short-stay management fees of 15% to 20% if Airbnb is permitted.
- Event-day noise tolerance near the stadium — affects long-let tenant profile.

| Strategy | Gross signal | Net risk |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Long-let professional | Near suburb average | Levy-heavy but stable |

Short-stay economics at NINEONS turn on permission before they turn on price. Launch marketing described the building as Airbnb and pet friendly, but the conduct rules registered by the body corporate decide what an owner may actually do, and a scheme of 23 sections can change those rules faster than a 200-unit tower. A special resolution under the STSMA needs 75% support, which in a building this small is a handful of owners acting together. Ask for the current conduct rules and the last two years of trustee minutes before you underwrite tourism income at all. Where short-stay is permitted, deduct management fees of 15% to 20% of gross before comparing anything to the 8.0% modelled gross on a Green Point long-let, then add a realistic seasonal void on top.

## Foreign buyer workflow on resale

Non-residents buy NINEONS resale on exactly the same terms as a local buyer, with no foreign surcharge anywhere in the transaction. The differences for foreign buyers are funding and exit. South African banks lend non-residents up to about 50% of price, so roughly half of a R4.5 million two-bedroom arrives from offshore through an authorised dealer.

Transfer duty applies on any resale here, following the SARS scale that charges nothing below R1,210,000, then 6% on the slice above R1,663,800 and 11% above R2,994,800, with no VAT because the seller is not a VAT-registered developer. On exit, section 35A obliges the conveyancer to withhold 7.5% of the price from a non-resident individual seller, 10% from a company and 15% from a trust, on any sale above R2 million.

1. Offer on live listing with suspensive bond clause if financing locally.
2. FICA through conveyancer; funds via authorised dealer.
3. Transfer duty on resale (no VAT on secondary sales).
4. Non-resident endorsement on title for repatriation.
5. Bond up to about 50% LTV for qualifying non-residents.
