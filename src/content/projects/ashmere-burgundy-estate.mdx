The design brief is domestic rather than investment-led: open-plan living spaces that carry a family through weekday evenings and weekend entertaining, and private gardens that give children and pets a safe outdoor area. Rabie positions it on access, with major routes close enough for a commute and enough distance for the estate to be quiet.

Burgundy Estate itself is the reason this reads differently from a new precinct. Rabie has been building here across several developments, which means the roads, the retail and the neighbours already exist. ## Why the price sits where it does

Because a house and a flat in the same estate are different products, and rent does not follow price.

| In Burgundy Estate | From | What it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| Finwood | R1.82m | Sectional title apartments |
| Ashmere | R3.45m | Three-bedroom houses with gardens |

A house at nearly double the entry price does not let for double the rent. That is the arithmetic that decides whether Ashmere is the right buy for you, and it points in a clear direction: this is a purchase for living in, or for a landlord who wants a long, stable family tenancy rather than a headline yield.

Insider tip: if you are weighing Ashmere against [Finwood](/projects/finwood-burgundy-estate/) purely as an investment, get live comparable rents for a three-bed house and a two-bed flat in the same estate before you decide. The gap in rent is almost always narrower than the gap in price, and that ratio is the whole argument.

## The advantages and disadvantages of the house over the flat

One is a lifestyle argument and one is arithmetic, and they point in opposite directions.

The advantages of a house are space, a private garden, and a tenant who stays for years because moving a family is expensive. The disadvantages are financial: a 2026 entry at R3.45m against R1.82m for a flat in the same estate, maintenance no body corporate absorbs, and a rent that does not rise in proportion to the price. If the purchase is for living in, the first list wins. If it is for letting, the second usually does.

## What a foreign buyer should know

The law is easy here. The financing is the constraint.

South Africa places no surcharge on foreign buyers and requires no visa to own property, so a non-resident buys an Ashmere house on the same basis as a citizen. Three practical points change the arithmetic:

1. **Lending is capped near 50% loan-to-value** for most non-residents, so on a R3.45m house the cash requirement is roughly R1.7m before costs, and instalments on the balance are conventionally modelled over a 20 years term.
2. **Buying from a VAT-registered developer a developer price includes 15% VAT and escapes duty altogether**, which is a different and usually cheaper tax position than a resale at the same price. The [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) shows what a resale would cost instead.
3. **Funds should enter through an authorised dealer bank** with the title endorsed non-resident, which costs nothing now and protects clean repatriation later.

The wider foreign buyer position is in the [foreigner guide](/guides/buy-cape-town-property-foreigner/), and the sequence from offer to registration is in [how to buy a house in Cape Town](/guides/buy-a-house-in-cape-town/).
