## What Clara Anna Fontein actually is

Not a development. An estate holding three developments, which is why the same search brings up prices that seem to contradict each other.

The land is 128 hectares of rolling hills above Durbanville, bordered on one side by a protected game reserve where animals come down to a watering hole inside the estate itself. The outlook is farmland and wine farms with the Overberg mountains behind, and the Durbanville CBD is a few minutes by car, which is the combination the estate sells: rural aspect, urban convenience.

| Product | Count | What you get | Tenure |
| --- | ---: | --- | --- |
| The Village townhouses | 126 | A completed home | Freehold title |
| Plot-and-plan erven | 344 | Land plus a build contract | Freehold title |
| Oasis Life retirement village | 125 | A home for your lifetime | Life right, not ownership |

That adds to about 595 homes at full build-out. Roughly 270 families live there now, so the estate is a little under half occupied and construction continues around residents, which matters if you are buying to live rather than to let.

Insider tip: ask which phase your erf or unit sits in relative to the remaining build programme. On a half-built estate the difference between a plot on a finished street and a plot facing three more years of construction traffic is real, and it never appears on a price list.

## Why the tenure decides the investment, not the finishes

Because two of the three products are property you own and one is not, and the marketing sits them side by side.

A townhouse or a plot-and-plan erf is freehold title. You own it, you can mortgage it, you can let it, you can sell it to anybody who wants it, and what you get on exit is whatever the market pays. That is the ordinary South African arrangement the [sectional title versus freehold guide](/guides/sectional-title-vs-freehold-cape-town/) sets out.

A life right at Oasis Life is a different instrument. You advance an interest-free loan to the owner and receive a real right to occupy a specific home for the rest of your life. The operator keeps ownership and keeps responsibility for the building. What returns to your estate when the right ends is governed by the life right agreement, not by the open market, and the [retirement village guide](/guides/retirement-village-cape-town/) works through what that contract normally contains.

Neither is better. They answer different questions. A buyer who wants an asset that compounds and passes to their children wants freehold. A buyer aged 60 or over who wants the maintenance, security and primary healthcare handled and does not need the capital back wants the life right. The mistake is buying one while reasoning about the other.

## What the estate gives you for the levy

Two clubhouses, and the second one is the reason the retirement village works.

The main Lifestyle Centre is shared by all residents: a clubhouse with a partly covered deck, a bar and a deli, boardroom and meeting rooms with uncapped wifi, a boma and a function room, two tennis courts, a squash court, a 25m lap pool, an exclusive-use gym, running and cycling paths, and playparks. Oasis Life residents have their own clubhouse on top of that, with a restaurant, braai facilities, a library and primary healthcare consulting rooms in the same building, and they also have access to the estate gym, pool, tennis and squash.

Security runs 24 hours with manned access control, biometric fingerprint identification, CCTV and a patrolled electrified perimeter. The [security estates guide](/guides/security-estates-cape-town-foreign-buyers/) covers what that infrastructure does to both price and levy.

All of it is paid for by a homeowners association levy, which sits on top of municipal rates and, on a sectional title townhouse, on top of the body corporate levy as well. Two levies and a rates bill on one home is normal on an estate like this and routinely surprises buyers who budgeted for one. The [rates and taxes guide](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) sets out the municipal side.

## Who actually lives here, and why that holds the price

Families with school-age children, and the school is next door.

Reddam House has a campus directly bordering the estate, teaching more than 500 learners. In the Cape Town northern suburbs, school catchment is one of the few forces that reliably holds residential demand through a soft market, because a family that has placed a child does not move for a rand. That is the same mechanism the [southern suburbs](/guides/southern-suburbs-cape-town-property/) trade on, transplanted north.

The second group is retirees, and the estate was deliberately built to hold both. Multi-generational is a marketing word most of the time; here it describes a site plan where a 125-unit retirement village sits inside a family estate with shared facilities, so grandparents can live a few hundred metres from grandchildren without sharing a house.

For a letting investor the consequence is specific: your tenant pool is families on school-linked leases, which means long tenancies, low turnover and rent that tracks the school year rather than the tourist season. That is the opposite of the [Atlantic Seaboard](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) pattern and it suits a different kind of owner.

## What plot-and-plan actually commits you to

More than a purchase. A building contract with a construction timeline attached.

Three hundred and forty-four of the homes here are plot-and-plan, meaning you buy the erf and contract to build a house to an approved design within the estate's architectural guidelines. The total cost is the land plus the build, and the build moves with your specification, so a quoted erf price is a floor rather than a number.

Four things change on this route compared with buying a finished house:

1. **The money goes out in draws**, against construction stages, rather than in one transfer, and a non-resident bond has to be structured for that.
2. **Transfer duty is paid on the land**, while the building contract carries VAT, so the tax works differently from a resale. The [transfer duty guide](/guides/south-africa-transfer-duty-explained/) and the [cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) cover which applies when.
3. **Architectural guidelines bind you**, which protects the estate's look and limits what you can build.
4. **The NHBRC warranty applies to the build**, and enrolment is worth confirming in writing before the first draw. The [NHBRC guide](/guides/nhbrc-warranty-south-africa-new-build/) explains what it does and does not cover.

Insider tip: on a half-built estate, get the remaining build programme in writing and check where your erf sits in it. Living on a construction site for two years is a cost that no price list carries.

## What to verify before you commit

Six things, and only the first one is unusual.

| Check | Why it matters | Where to get it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which product you are actually buying | Freehold and life rights behave differently on exit | The sale agreement, not the brochure |
| Current HOA levy and what it covers | Two levies plus rates is normal here | Homeowners association |
| Remaining build programme | Decides whether you live beside construction | Estate management |
| Architectural guidelines, if plot-and-plan | Binds your build cost | Estate documents |
| Life right agreement terms, if Oasis Life | Governs what comes back on exit | Oasis Life, read with your own attorney |
| Bond structure for a non-resident | Draw-downs on a build differ from a transfer | Your bank, before you sign |

Two numbers decide what a foreign buyer can actually do here. South African banks typically cap non-residents near 50% loan-to-value, so the cash requirement on a plot-and-plan build is roughly double a local buyer's on the same erf, and the build contract carries 15% VAT while the land carries transfer duty, so the tax lands in two places rather than one. The life rights product sits outside both: it is not a property transfer, so no duty arises, and it cannot be bonded. Oasis Life qualifies buyers at 60 years and over, and the brand launched in 2018, so the retirement component here is younger than the estate around it.

At about 270 of roughly 595 homes occupied, the estate is a little over 45% built out, which is the number to hold in mind when a sales agent describes the community as established.
