## How should you underwrite Granger Bay?

Granger Bay sits between the clock-tower Waterfront and Green Point's stadium corridor — the last major piece of harbour edge where the V&A can still grow without leaving the Atlantic Seaboard orbit. Growthpoint and PIC frame the project as shore protection plus economic development: breakwaters stabilise the coastline while reclaimed land hosts mixed-use towers, hotels, retail, and public promenades tied into existing pedestrian grids.

For property investors, Granger Bay is **optionality**, not inventory. Until marine reclamation clears Parliament and environmental law, there is no sectional title register to diligence. The actionable trade today is understanding how R24 billion of proposed amenity reshapes rents and liquidity in [Green Point](/areas/green-point-property-investment/) and completed [Silo District](/projects/silo-district-residences/) stock, while refusing to capitalise unapproved land into current offers.

Our [news analysis](/news/va-waterfront-granger-bay-development/) tracks approval milestones; this project brief translates those milestones into investor decision rules.

## What numbers define Granger Bay proposal in 2026?

Four figures carry every Granger Bay headline, and each is sponsor modelling rather than a transacted market: a R24 billion budget, 3.8 reclaimed hectares, R8.8 billion of modelled GDP and 27,000 modelled jobs, with the first hard approval gate cited around late 2027. Read them as scale and political weight, not as return.

| Metric | Cited figure | What it tells an investor |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Development budget | ~R24 billion | Institutional scale spread across a multi-year build |
| Reclaimed footprint | ~3.8 hectares, about 38,000 sqm | Small beside the existing V&A, enough for thousands of doors |
| Modelled GDP impact | ~R8.8 billion | The sponsors' economic case, not a return on your unit |
| Modelled job creation | ~27,000 | The approval argument, weighted toward construction phases |
| First hard approval gate | Around late 2027 | Nothing before this date is a secured asset |

The number missing from that list is the one that would matter most to a buyer: a price per square metre on an approved, registrable unit. It does not exist yet and cannot until parliamentary and environmental authorisation clears and a sectional title register opens, which realistically sits 12 to 24 months after approval, with residential phases typically trailing hospitality by 24 to 36 months. Full build-out belongs on a 5 to 10 year horizon.

The practical consequence is that any 2026 conversation about Granger Bay pricing is really a conversation about Green Point and Silo District comparables. Underwrite those on their current rents and levies, then treat the R24 billion as a scenario input: amenity uplift on one side, competing supply on the other.

## Approval pathway and why timing matters

Marine reclamation at Granger Bay crosses parliamentary approval, environmental impact assessment and coastal management scrutiny, with the first hard gate cited around late 2027. Three clocks run separately, and a sectional title register realistically opens 12 to 24 months after approval. Marketing that blends the three clocks into one date is a red flag.

- Policy approval: parliamentary and environmental authorisation
- Financial close: sponsors committing capital and appointing contractors
- First occupational certificates: years later, and the only date that carries rent

Public participation periods can shift schedules even when sponsors remain committed.

Until approval, treat Granger Bay like infrastructure equity: you monitor milestones, you do not underwrite rent on imaginary towers.

## What are the pros, cons and should track Granger Bay?

Granger Bay is optionality rather than inventory, and the balance sheet of arguments reflects that. The upside is institutional sponsorship and a R24 billion public realm on the harbour edge. The downside is that nothing is registrable before approval around late 2027, with sectional title registers opening 12 to 24 months after that.

- Institutional sponsors with decades of Waterfront delivery.
- Strengthens Cape Town's global harbour brand.
- Potential first-mover informational edge for adjacent owners.
- Public realm and breakwater narrative supports long-term precinct quality.

**Cons**

- Not investable real estate until approvals and registers exist.
- Approval and litigation risk can delay or shrink scope.
- Future supply may compress yields nearby before you benefit from amenity.
- Pre-sales on unapproved land are hazardous for foreign buyers far from site.

**The adjacent owner** monitors milestones to stress-test hold assumptions.

**The trophy buyer waiting for Waterfront stock** compares completed Silo resales now versus pipeline wait risk.

**The income investor** should ignore Granger Bay for yield and buy [Century City](/guides/century-city-property-investment-guide/) instead.

**The foreign speculator** must avoid non-refundable reservations without environmental and parliamentary sign-off.

## Granger Bay numbers foreign buyers should cite carefully

Sponsor numbers are scale indicators rather than returns. The R24 billion budget, the R8.8 billion GDP figure and the 27,000 job estimate all come from economic modelling attached to the reclamation proposal, and the 3.8 hectare footprint is roughly 38,000 sqm of developable land once reclamation completes.

- R24 billion budget: institutional scale across a multi-year build
- R8.8 billion GDP and 27,000 jobs: the approval argument, not your return
- 38,000 sqm reclaimed: enough for thousands of keys and doors over a decade

That footprint is a fraction of the existing V&A but large enough to add thousands of hotel keys and residential doors over a decade-long build-out. Use sponsor numbers in scenario conversations with advisers; do not capitalise them into a 10-year DCF on Green Point stock you already own.

## How to track Granger Bay without overpaying today

Set calendar reminders for parliamentary committee dates, environmental appeal windows, and Growthpoint annual results presentations each March and September. When approval lands, expect a 12 to 24 month gap before credible primary residential pricing appears with NHBRC enrolment and sectional title registers you can audit. Until then, the highest-conviction Waterfront buys remain completed [Silo](/projects/silo-district-residences/) resales with observable levies, or [Green Point](/areas/green-point-property-investment/) apartments with stadium and promenade walkability at lower ticket sizes than Silo penthouses.

Foreign buyers monitoring from London, Amsterdam, or Dubai should pair this brief with the [Atlantic Seaboard investment guide](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) so Granger Bay headlines do not pull you into unapproved deposits while better-documented stock is available elsewhere in Cape Town.

## Scenario workbook for nearby Green Point owners

Scenario workbook for a Green Point owner is two runs of the same net yield model: one adding competing supply from a R24 billion precinct, one adding amenity uplift from it. Only the spread between the two runs should move a hold or sell decision, and neither belongs in a 10-year DCF as the base case.

## What red flags should pause this Cape Town purchase?

Red flags on a pipeline scheme are mostly paperwork that does not exist yet. A sales mandate that cannot show parliamentary or environmental approval references, a yield projection on unbuilt units, and a deposit routed to a personal account rather than a conveyancer's trust are the three that should stop a Granger Bay conversation before the R24 billion headline does any work.

- Sales mandates that cannot show parliamentary or environmental approval references.
- Yield projections on unbuilt units without completed comparables nearby.
- Deposits into personal accounts rather than conveyancer trust.
- Marketing that treats 2027 approval as guaranteed construction start.
- Failure to disclose marine construction timeline and noise impacts on existing tenants.
