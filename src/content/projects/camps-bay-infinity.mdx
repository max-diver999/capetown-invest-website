## Design and amenity stack

Darryl Croome Architects designed six three-bedroom apartments across a size band of roughly 276 to 375 square metres, which is house-scale floor area stacked on a coastal site.

The specification list from the 2016 launch is the one that matters to a resale buyer: private rim-flow pools on each terrace, a gym, 24-hour security, SMEG kitchens, and double glazing throughout. Double glazing is not a luxury flourish on this stretch of Victoria Road. The south-easter runs hard across Bakoven in summer and the road itself carries constant traffic, so glazing quality is what separates a terrace you use from one you look at.

The amenity stack cuts both ways for an investor. Six units means low churn and quick decisions. It also means the shared plant, the lift, the security contract, and common-area maintenance are divided six ways rather than sixty, so the levy per unit is structurally high and a single deferred repair lands as a visible special levy. Private pools compound this: each is a maintenance liability inside a building approaching a decade old, and waterproofing cycles on a terrace pool above occupied space are expensive to get wrong.

What to ask for before you offer: the managing agent's levy history over three years, the ten-year maintenance plan with the reserve fund balance against it, any special levy raised since completion, and the insurance schedule showing wind and water damage cover on the terraces. Design quality is verifiable from the architect's drawings. Whether the body corporate has funded the upkeep of that design is only verifiable in the financials.

## Resale underwriting on Victoria Road

Model carrying cost honestly before you model rent, because on a R25 million Bakoven apartment the monthly outgoings decide the return long before the tenant does. A six-unit scheme divides every shared cost six ways, terrace pools carry their own maintenance line, and coastal municipal accounts run high at trophy valuations. Four lines to price before you make an offer:

- Body corporate levy: pull from seller or managing agent — not published in launch-era press.
- Municipal rates on coastal accounts: often R3,000 to R8,000+ monthly at trophy size.
- Insurance on terrace pools and high wind exposure.
- Special levy risk if lift or pool plant deferred.

Subtract void months before you call any of this yield. A R25 million ticket carrying roughly R15,000 a month before bond service needs exceptional rent simply to clear 3% net, which is why buyers on this strip treat the 4.4% modelled net on mainstream Camps Bay stock as a ceiling rather than a floor. Municipal rates form part of that carry and are billed to the owner rather than the body corporate: the City exempts the first R620,000 of valuation and charges roughly 0.64 cents per rand a year above that, which on a trophy valuation is a five-figure annual line on its own. Add insurance loaded for wind and terrace-pool water exposure, then a seasonal void allowance if you let short-stay. Underwrite Infinity as capital preservation with a rental offset, never as an income asset.

## Foreign buyer mechanics on trophy resale

Sellers marketing trophy Atlantic Seaboard stock often quote dollar prices, so rebuild your home-currency exit at offer stage rather than at sale. Rand depreciation flattered 2016 launch buyers, and future repatriation depends entirely on the rate on the day you sell. The tax layer is the other half of that exit. Section 35A obliges the conveyancer to withhold 7.5% of the price where a non-resident individual sells above R2 million, 10% for a company and 15% for a trust, credited against the final capital gains assessment rather than kept. Holding through a trust or company raises the CGT inclusion rate from 40% to 80%, which on a R25 million asset is the difference between two very different tax bills. Local banks lend non-residents up to about 50% of price, so roughly half the ticket crosses the border and must be recorded on the way in.

## Lift, pool, and common-area replacement cycle

Six-unit buildings still face lift motor replacement every 15 to 20 years and pool plant refurbishment every 8 to 10 years. A special levy of R200,000 split six ways is material — read reserve fund balances before you unconditional.
