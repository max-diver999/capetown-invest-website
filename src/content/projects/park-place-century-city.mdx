## What Park Place is

Three buildings, a clubhouse, and an orientation decision applied to every unit.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Century City, facing Ratanga Park |
| Developer | Rabie Property Group |
| Architects | Vivid Architects |
| Composition | Three residential buildings plus a clubhouse with pool |
| From | R6.8m |
| Remaining | 2 penthouse units |

Every apartment and penthouse carries a balcony or terrace, and every one faces north. That is a scheme-wide decision rather than a feature of the better units, which is unusual: most developments sell a north-facing premium to some buyers and give the rest an east or west aspect.

The garden setting faces the waterways, trees and lawns of Ratanga Park, and from the higher floors the view extends to Lion's Head and Signal Hill. The [Century City guide](/guides/century-city-property-investment-guide/) covers the precinct.

## Why north-facing is the specification that matters here

Because it decides whether the outdoor space is usable in the season you would want it.

Two effects, and neither is aesthetic:

1. **Light.** North is the sun side in the southern hemisphere, so a north-facing room takes sun through the day rather than for one part of it.
2. **Wind.** The summer south-easter runs from roughly November to February and is the thing that empties Cape Town balconies. Orientation away from it converts a terrace from a decoration into a room you use.

Insider tip: when comparing two apartments in the same precinct, ask which way the balcony faces before asking what the finishes are. Finishes get replaced in fifteen years; aspect does not change for the life of the building.

## What is actually left, and what buying it costs

Two penthouses, which is the thinnest possible market.

At R6.8m and above, the buyer is in a segment where each Century City scheme holds only a handful of comparable units. That cuts both ways. Scarcity supports the price while the building is new, and it makes valuation harder when you come to sell, because there may be no recent comparable transaction in the same building to point at.

The route question matters as much as the unit. A developer sale from a VAT-registered developer a developer sale wraps 15% VAT into the asking figure and attracts no duty at all. A later resale at this level attracts duty on the upper bands of the SARS scale plus conveyancing, which the [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) sets out. A non-resident meets a further limit, because lenders here rarely go past 50% of value for a non-resident, which at R6.8m means roughly R3.4m in cash before costs.

## The advantages, and the disadvantages that come with them

Both sides of this scheme follow from the same two decisions.

The advantages are the ones that survive a decade: a north aspect on every unit, a park frontage that no later release can take, and a clubhouse with a pool that a three-building scheme can support and a single block cannot. The disadvantages follow from the price. At R6.8m the buyer pool narrows sharply, penthouses are a handful of units in any one building, and the resale comparable set is correspondingly thin. A 2026 buyer is also arriving into a precinct that has been releasing phases since the early 2000s, so the supply question is structural rather than temporary.
