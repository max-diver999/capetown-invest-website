Quick answer: Mont Reve is a 116-unit building over eight floors in Sea Point, finished in 2025 by Berman Brothers, and it is both sold out and fully let. Anyone searching for it now is looking at a resale or a sublet. The feature that should shape the decision is not the finish level but the hotel operation inside the building, which changes how the scheme is governed and how its costs are shared.

A mixed building, and the mix is the point rather than an afterthought.

The retail component is worth pausing on. A Sea Point block with 1,500 square metres of curated shopping and dining at its base, including the return of New York Bagels, is contributing to the street rather than presenting a garage door to it. In a corridor where much of the older stock meets the pavement with parking, that is a durable advantage and one of the few building-level features that reliably survives into resale value.

## What the hotel inside the building actually changes

Two populations, two rhythms, and a governance structure that has to hold both.

Mont Reve runs a hotel operation alongside the apartments, with concierge services and fully furnished residences for guests. Rather than pretending the two groups are the same, the building separates the amenity:

| Pool | Floor | Who uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Hotel pool | Second | Hotel guests |
| Residents' pool | Seventh | Apartment residents |
| Penthouse pool | Eighth | Penthouse residents |

That is a deliberate design answer to the standard complaint in mixed schemes, which is that residents end up sharing their facilities with a rotating population of visitors. Segregating by floor and user group is more expensive to build than one shared pool and it removes the friction that usually surfaces at the third AGM.

For a buyer the questions that remain are financial and constitutional rather than recreational:

1. **How are costs apportioned** between the hotel and residential components, and does the split reflect actual usage of lifts, security and common areas?
2. **How does voting work** where two very different owner interests sit in one scheme?
3. **What happens if the hotel operator changes**, and what does the scheme's structure oblige a successor to do?

None of that is in a brochure. It is in the sectional title register, the conduct rules and the body corporate budget, and the [body corporate due diligence guide](/guides/body-corporate-due-diligence-cape-town/) sets out how to read them.

Insider tip: in a building with a commercial operator inside it, ask for the last two AGM minutes rather than only the current budget. Minutes show you what the two owner groups have actually argued about, which is the best available forecast of what they will argue about next.

## Buying now means buying from an owner, not a developer

Which changes the cost stack before it changes anything else.

The developer sale is gone: Berman Brothers marks the scheme sold out and fully let. So the routes in are a resale from an existing owner or, for a tenant, a sublet when one appears.

The financial consequence is the same one that applies across sold-out schemes and is routinely missed. A developer sale from a VAT-registered developer carries VAT inside the price and no transfer duty. A private resale carries transfer duty on the SARS scale and no VAT. On a Sea Point apartment those are materially different numbers, and the [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) works out the difference at any price. The [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-property-cape-town-guide/) covers cessions and resales as routes.

The size of that gap is worth stating. A developer sale carries 15% VAT inside the price and no transfer duty; a private resale carries duty on the SARS scale and no VAT, and at Sea Point prices that lands in the upper bands rather than the entry ones. A non-resident meets a second limit at the same time, because South African banks typically cap lending near 50% loan-to-value, which moves the cash requirement rather than the purchase cost.

The second consequence is availability. A fully let building with 116 units produces a thin and irregular supply of resales, so the practical approach is to register interest with agents active in the block rather than to wait for a portal listing.

## Whether you can short-let your own unit

A separate question from the hotel, and decided elsewhere.

The presence of a professional hotel operation in the building tells you nothing about whether an individual owner may list their apartment on a nightly platform. That is decided by two things: the conduct rules registered against the sectional title scheme, and the City of Cape Town by-law position.

A body corporate can restrict or prohibit short stays by special resolution, which the [body corporate Airbnb rules guide](/guides/body-corporate-airbnb-ban-rules/) explains, and the City's registration requirements sit on top, which the [short-term rental rules guide](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-cape-town/) covers. In a building that already earns from hospitality through a dedicated operator, there is an obvious commercial reason for the scheme to restrict owners from competing with it, so this is a question to settle in writing before you buy rather than after.

## Where this sits in the Sea Point market

In the newest tier of a corridor whose stock is mostly decades old.

Sea Point is the densest apartment market in Cape Town and one of the few places where a foreign buyer, a local investor and a long-term tenant all want the same building. Most of the supply is older, which means a 2025 completion with modern services, secure parking and air-conditioning is competing on a small field. The [Sea Point page](/areas/sea-point-property-investment/) covers the local market and the [Atlantic Seaboard guide](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) places it in the corridor.

For the developer's wider record, including the other two Sea Point schemes it has delivered, see the [Berman Brothers page](/developers/berman-brothers/). Levies are the line that decides net yield on any sectional title purchase here, and the [levies guide](/guides/sectional-title-levies-cape-town/) sets out what to ask for.
