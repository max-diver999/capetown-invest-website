## What Oasis Life is selling

A lifetime of living arrangements, bought with capital, in a specific house on a specific estate.

Oasis Life launched in 2018 as Rabie's retirement brand and now runs four estates across the Western Cape: Burgundy Estate, Clara Anna Fontein in Durbanville, Constantia in the southern suburbs and Sunningdale on the West Coast corridor. The common design is a hospitality-led estate for residents aged 60 and over, with a Lifestyle Centre or Clubhouse built in the first phase rather than promised for a later one.

That sequencing decision matters more than it sounds. On many retirement developments the communal building arrives with the final phase, which means the first three years of residents buy a community that does not exist yet. Delivering it first means the deli, the activities and the primary healthcare consulting rooms are running from the day the earliest residents move in.

Insider tip: ask which phase the Lifestyle Centre is in on any retirement scheme you look at, not just this one. It is the single question that separates a village from a housing estate with a clubhouse pencilled in.

## How the life rights structure works

Four parties, one interest-free loan, and a real right that lasts as long as you do.

| Party | What they do |
| --- | --- |
| The Owner | Owns the property and grants the housing interest over it |
| The Life Right Holder | Receives the right, in exchange for an interest-free loan to the Owner |
| The Payer | Pays the loan amount, and may be the holder or a third party such as a child |
| The Occupant | The holder, or the holder's tenant |

The housing interest grants a real right to use and occupy the property for the duration of the holder's life. Two people can hold one right together, which is how a couple is normally accommodated. The right can end when the holder decides to move out, and it ends on death.

The word doing the work in all of this is *loan*. Your capital goes across as an interest-free loan rather than as a purchase price, and the counter-performance you receive is occupation rather than title. Everything that follows from that, including what comes back at the end, is set by the agreement between you and the owner.

## Why this is not a property investment, and why that can still be the right choice

Because the two products answer different questions, and only one of them is about money compounding.

| | Freehold or sectional title | Life right |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Who owns the home | You | The operator |
| Can you mortgage it | Yes | No, it is not your property |
| Who maintains the building | You, or the body corporate you pay | The operator |
| Who can buy it from you | Anyone | Governed by the agreement |
| What sets your exit value | The market | The life right agreement |

A buyer at 65 with capital, no wish to manage a roof, and a preference for having primary healthcare on the same site is buying the right thing. A buyer who needs the capital to grow and pass on is not, and no amount of finish quality changes that. The comparison people reach for, and should not, is a price per square metre against ordinary stock. Those numbers are not measuring the same thing. ## What to read before you sign

Five documents, and the order matters.

1. **The life right agreement**, in full, with your own attorney rather than the operator's. This is the document that decides what happens to your capital.
2. **The exit calculation, demonstrated on your own numbers.** Ask for a worked example on the amount you would actually advance, not a percentage in a brochure.
3. **The monthly levy and what it covers**, including which services are inside it and which are billed separately.
4. **The healthcare arrangement**, specifically what the on-site primary healthcare includes and what happens if care needs rise beyond it.
5. **The estate rules on letting**, because the structure allows an occupant who is the holder's tenant, and whether that is practically available to you is a contract question.

It is worth being precise about what the life rights structure removes as well as what it gives. Because the capital moves as a loan rather than a purchase price, no transfer duty arises, and the 15% VAT that would apply to a new build from a VAT-registered developer does not apply either. The other side of that is finance: a bond secures a property, and you are not buying one, so the 50% loan-to-value that a South African bank might extend to a non-resident on an ordinary purchase is simply not available. A life right is a cash transaction. Oasis Life qualifies buyers at 60 years and over.

Nothing on this page is a substitute for that agreement. A life right is a long contract with a small number of clauses that decide everything, and the clauses that decide everything are not the ones about the deli.
