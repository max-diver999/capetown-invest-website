## What Finwood is, and why the word final matters

An apartment development, and the last of its kind in this estate.

| --- | --- |

Rabie states plainly that this is the final sectional title development in Burgundy Estate. That is a supply statement rather than a marketing one, and it has a consequence worth naming: after Finwood, a buyer who wants a new-build flat in this precinct has nowhere to go except the resale market.

Scarcity of that kind supports resale pricing. It does not guarantee it, and it applies only inside the estate, not to the northern suburbs generally, where other developers keep releasing. Treat it as one factor rather than as a thesis. The [northern suburbs guide](/guides/northern-suburbs-cape-town-property/) covers the wider corridor.

The move from R1.24m at [Rhapsody](/projects/rhapsody-burgundy-estate/) to R1.82m at Finwood is a rough read on what new-build pricing has done in this precinct between the two releases. It is directional rather than an index, because the unit mixes differ, but it is the closest thing to a like-for-like the estate offers.

Insider tip: on a final-phase release, ask what is actually left rather than what the range starts at. A published starting price usually belongs to the unit type that sold first, and the remaining stock in a final phase is rarely the cheapest.

## What decides the return here

The net, and on a new scheme the net is partly a forecast.

Finwood is the entry point in Burgundy Estate, which makes it the stronger yield candidate against [Ashmere](/projects/ashmere-burgundy-estate/) on arithmetic alone: a R1.82m flat does not let for half of what a R3.45m house lets for, so the ratio favours the flat. Four costs stand between gross and net:

1. **The levy**, which on a new sectional title scheme launches as a body corporate projection rather than a known figure.
2. **Municipal rates**, billed against the valuation roll rather than the price you paid.
3. **Management**, which runs at 8% to 10% of rent for a long-term letting agent.
4. **Vacancy**, where one month in 12 months is the conservative starting assumption on new stock.

The [levies guide](/guides/sectional-title-levies-cape-town/) sets out what to ask the body corporate for, and the [long-term rental guide](/guides/long-term-rental-cape-town-guide/) covers what tenants in this corridor actually pay.

## Buying off-plan on a final phase

Two of the numbers you are quoted are estimates until handover.

The completion date in a brochure is a target; the one in the contract is the one that binds. The levy quoted at signature is a projection; the levy in year three is whatever the scheme's actual costs turn out to be. Neither of those is a criticism of the developer, it is simply how a new scheme works, and the [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-property-cape-town-guide/) covers the full sequence from reservation to transfer.

Two tax points are worth having straight before you sign. Buying from a VAT-registered developer a first sale by the developer carries 15% VAT within the price, with duty falling away entirely, which is normally the cheaper position; a later resale of the same flat at R1.82m falls into the 3% transfer duty band with the conveyancing stack restored. And a non-resident meets a separate limit, because South African banks typically lend near 50% loan-to-value, which moves the cash requirement rather than the price.

Confirm the NHBRC enrolment in writing before the first payment, which the [NHBRC guide](/guides/nhbrc-warranty-south-africa-new-build/) explains, and read the registered conduct rules on short stays and pets rather than assuming either.
