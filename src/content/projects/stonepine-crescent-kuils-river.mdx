## The two prices, and why we are not choosing between them

Because the developer publishes both, on the same page, at the same time.

| Where it appears | Figure |
| --- | ---: |
| Descriptive text | Homes starting at R2.25 million |
| Selling-from banner | R2.9m |

The likely explanation is straightforward: R2.25m belonged to an earlier phase and R2.9m is phase two, which is the release now on sale. That is a reasonable inference and it is not a verified fact, so it is written here as an inference rather than as the answer.

The practical instruction is the same either way. Ask the sales office which figure applies to the homes actually available now, and get it on the price list rather than in conversation. A R650,000 gap between two published numbers is not a rounding difference.

Insider tip: when a developer's own page disagrees with itself on price, that is usually a phase boundary rather than an error. Ask what changed between the phases, because the answer is often specification rather than only inflation.

## What the homes are

Freestanding houses, which is unusual at this price in a new estate.

Two and three-bedroom freestanding homes, in single-storey and duplex forms, with classic and modern architecture in a neutral palette of white, grey and stone chosen to sit against the natural surroundings. The estate is gated and pet-friendly, on Neethlingshof street in Kuils River.

Freestanding rather than sectional title changes three things:

1. **No body corporate levy**, because there is no common property to run, though a homeowners association levy still covers estate security and shared space.
2. **No special resolutions** deciding what you may do with your own home, within the estate's architectural rules.
3. **All maintenance is yours.** The roof, the geyser and the boundary wall come off your budget rather than a scheme's reserve fund.

The [sectional title versus freehold guide](/guides/sectional-title-vs-freehold-cape-town/) works through what that ownership difference means in practice, and the [security estates guide](/guides/security-estates-cape-town-foreign-buyers/) covers what the estate infrastructure costs to run.

## Who lives in Kuils River, and what that means for letting

Families on long leases, and nothing seasonal.

Kuils River sits inland and east in the northern suburbs corridor alongside Durbanville, Bellville and Brackenfell. It is a family market priced well below the coastal corridors, and the constraint that shapes it is the commute rather than the beach.

For a letting investor that produces a specific profile: multi-year leases, low turnover, no summer peak and no short-let opportunity worth modelling. That is the opposite of the [Atlantic Seaboard](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) pattern, and it suits an owner who wants predictability rather than a high headline gross. Model the return on net: a managing agent takes 8% to 10% of rent and one vacant month in 12 months is the conservative allowance. The [long-term rental guide](/guides/long-term-rental-cape-town-guide/) covers what those tenants pay, and the [northern suburbs guide](/guides/northern-suburbs-cape-town-property/) covers the corridor.

A freestanding house also lets differently from a flat. Families rent houses for longer, and they expect the landlord to maintain a garden and a roof rather than a body corporate to do it.

## Advantages and disadvantages of a freestanding estate home

The same feature produces both lists.

The advantages: no body corporate levy, no special resolution deciding what you may do with your own home, a garden that is genuinely yours, and a 2026 entry price well below the coastal corridors for a whole house rather than a flat. The disadvantages: every maintenance item is yours to fund, from the roof to the geyser, a homeowners association levy still runs alongside municipal rates, and the commute is the constraint that decides your tenant pool. A house also takes longer to sell than a flat in a soft market, because the buyer pool is smaller.

## The costs around the price

Two taxes and a lending cap, plus a levy that never stops.

Buying from a VAT-registered developer the developer's price already contains 15% VAT, and no duty is charged on top. A later resale at R2.9m falls into the 8% transfer duty band with conveyancing restored, and the [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) shows what that costs. A non-resident meets the usual limit, because local banks seldom fund more than half the value for a foreign buyer.

The homeowners association levy and municipal rates both continue for as long as you own the house, and rates are billed against the municipal valuation rather than what you paid, which the [rates and taxes guide](/guides/cape-town-rates-taxes-property/) explains. Confirm the NHBRC enrolment in writing before the first payment on any new build.
