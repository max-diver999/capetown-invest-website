## UDZ tax mechanics (South African buyers)

Venice House falls inside Cape Town's Urban Development Zone. South African taxpayers may claim accelerated depreciation on qualifying building costs — potentially material to after-tax cash flow in early ownership years. Building-versus-land split, purchase date, and current SARS schedules determine the allowance.

Foreign buyers with no South African taxable income should underwrite **zero UDZ benefit** unless a tax adviser structures otherwise. Do not pay a Burg Street premium for tax you cannot use.

## Golden Acre rental supply context

The Golden Acre site sits within about two kilometres of Burg Street, and redevelopment there matters to a Venice House owner for one reason: new residential supply competes for the same tenant and the same guest. The CBD is small enough that a large scheme two kilometres away is not a different market. It is the next listing your prospective tenant opens.

Supply pressure works through rent rather than price first. When a new building releases a block of units into the letting market at once, the operator prices to fill, which caps what surrounding stock can ask for a season or two. Vacancy rises before rent falls, so watch days-on-market in City Bowl listings as the early signal rather than waiting for advertised rents to move. The [vacancy rate guide](/guides/cape-town-vacancy-rates-rental/) sets out how to read that.

There is a counterweight worth stating fairly. Venice House is a boutique 36-unit heritage rebuild with a retained facade, concierge, and a ground-floor coffee shop, and that product does not compete head to head with a large modern block. Buyers and guests who want character and scale scarcity will not substitute. But the studio end of the building, where the tenant is price-sensitive and the unit is a commodity, is exposed.

Practical response: do not underwrite rent escalation above inflation on a studio while a significant pipeline is unbuilt nearby, check the current status of any announced scheme with the City rather than from press coverage, and keep a void allowance in the model that survives a competitive letting season.

## Prospekt handover and defects window

Venice House completed around Q4 2024, which means the defect and warranty clocks started for original purchasers, not for you. That distinction is the single most important thing a resale buyer on this building needs to understand. NHBRC cover on a new build runs roughly three months for general defects from occupation, twelve months for roof leaks, and five years for major structural failure, and those periods run from the original handover date. Buying in 2026 means the short windows are long gone and only the tail of the structural cover may remain.

So ask two questions of the seller and the managing agent. First, what snag list was raised at handover and was it closed out, in writing? Unresolved snags on a unit tend to resurface as your maintenance bill. Second, has the body corporate lodged any claim or complaint with the developer or the NHBRC on communal elements such as waterproofing, lifts, or the retained heritage facade? A heritage rebuild carries more interface risk between old fabric and new structure than a clean new build does, and communal defects land on the levy roll rather than on one owner.

Then read the body corporate minutes from the first two years of operation. That period, immediately after handover, is where structural or systems problems surface and where trustees decide whether to pursue the developer or simply raise a levy. The [NHBRC warranty guide](/guides/nhbrc-warranty-south-africa-new-build/) covers what remains claimable, and a [snagging inspection](/guides/snagging-inspection-new-build-cape-town/) is still worth commissioning on resale even though the building is no longer new.
