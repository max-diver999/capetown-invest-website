Three unit types with published floors, and a release that is moving.

| Two bedroom | One or two | R1,909,900 |

Balwin marks a new phase as released for sale while the individual unit types carry a releasing-soon note, which usually means the current release is partly taken and the next tranche is close. Ask which units are on the live list rather than budgeting from the published floor, because on a moving release the entry figure often belongs to stock that has gone.

The developer covering attorney fees and transfer costs is a genuine saving rather than a marketing line, and it does not survive into a later private resale. On a resale at R1,179,900 the buyer meets the 3% transfer duty band plus the full conveyancing stack, which the [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) prices out.

## The contradiction worth naming

Balwin sells this estate to rental investors and closes half the rental market.

The developer's own positioning lists rental investors alongside first-time buyers and young families as the intended market. The same page prohibits short-term letting across the scheme. Both statements are true and they sit oddly together, so an investor should read them as one instruction rather than two facts.

What it means in practice:

1. **Nightly-rate income is not available**, so any yield borrowed from a Sea Point or City Bowl short-let comparison is irrelevant here. The [body corporate Airbnb rules guide](/guides/body-corporate-airbnb-ban-rules/) covers how such rules bind, and [Suikerbos](/projects/suikerbos-balwin/) carries Balwin's own wording of what counts as a short stay.
2. **Long-term letting is the whole model**, which in the Helderberg means families and local workers on multi-year leases.
3. **The restriction is a feature for the owner-occupier majority**, because a building with no nightly turnover has quieter corridors and lower common-area wear, and the developer is plainly building for that majority.

Insider tip: for a Balwin scheme, check whether the letting rule is registered in the conduct rules against the sectional title scheme or sits only in your sale agreement. A registered rule binds every future owner and therefore every future buyer of your unit; a contractual one binds you.

## Where Somerset West actually is

In the Helderberg, roughly 45 km from the Cape Town CBD, and that distance shapes everything.

Balwin markets the scheme under a Cape Town banner while describing its setting as Somerset West. Both are defensible, and only one is useful to a buyer. Somerset West is a Helderberg town with its own centre, schools and employment, not a commuter suburb of the city, and a tenant who signs expecting a short city commute does not renew.

For a landlord that produces a specific and rather good profile: local demand, low turnover, and no exposure to the summer visitor cycle that drives coastal rents up and then empties them in June. The [Somerset West page](/areas/somerset-west-property-investment/) covers the local market, and the [long-term rental guide](/guides/long-term-rental-cape-town-guide/) covers what the leases look like.

## The advantages, and the disadvantages that come with them

Both follow from the same positioning.

The advantages are a genuinely low entry at R1,179,900 for a new-build one-bedroom with 24/7 security and eco-friendly appliances, a developer covering the transfer cost stack, and a tenant market that behaves predictably across a 2026 letting year. The disadvantages are that short lets are closed, the Helderberg rental market is thinner than the metro so a vacancy takes longer to fill, and a Balwin estate sells many near-identical units into the same suburb, which means your unit competes with its neighbours when several leases end together.

## What decides the net

Four costs, and one of them is a projection when you sign.

Letting agents charge 8% to 10% of the rent they collect, budget a month empty across a 12 months cycle, municipal rates run against the valuation roll rather than the purchase price, and the body corporate levy on a new scheme launches as a budget projection rather than a settled figure. The [levies guide](/guides/sectional-title-levies-cape-town/) sets out what to ask the body corporate for, and the [buy-to-let mortgage guide](/guides/buy-to-let-cape-town-mortgage/) covers what banks will fund.

Two financing points for a foreign buyer. Purchasing new from the developer means the price already carries 15% VAT with no duty on top, which is usually the cheaper of the two tax routes. And lending to a non-resident is generally held at about 50% of value, so the cash requirement at the one-bedroom entry is roughly R590,000 before costs.
