## The levy is the whole argument

Amenity-heavy precincts carry structurally higher levies than plain residential blocks, and Harbour Arch is amenity-heavy by design. Request two years of audited body corporate financials, the parking bay allocation showing whether a bay is included or bought separately, and any special levy history tied to pool, lift, or fire-compliance upgrades.

Parking is the line most often mispriced at Harbour Arch: about 1,200 bays serve roughly 560 apartments, yet not every unit includes a bay in its base price, and a bay held as monthly hire rather than as an exclusive use area can be repriced by the trustees at will. Establish in writing whether your bay is a separate section on the title deed, an exclusive use area registered against the scheme, or a hire arrangement, because only the first two survive a resale intact. Municipal rates sit outside the levy entirely and are billed to the owner, the City taking nothing on the first R620,000 of valuation and 0.0064 in the rand a year on everything above it. Under the STSMA a special levy needs a 75% special resolution, so it surfaces in trustee minutes long before it reaches your statement.

## How do foreign buyers complete a Harbour Arch purchase?

Non-residents buy Harbour Arch like any other sectional title product, with no buyer surcharge and a local bond capped near 50% of value. The sequence is fixed: establish whether the unit is primary stock carrying 15% VAT or resale carrying transfer duty above R1,210,000, then run FICA through the conveyancer and introduce offshore funds through an authorised dealer.

Non-residents buy Harbour Arch like any sectional title product:

1. Identify primary vs resale — developer stock may be VAT-inclusive; resale attracts transfer duty.
2. Offer with suspensive bond clause if financing locally up to about 50% LTV.
3. FICA through conveyancer; offshore funds via authorised dealer.
4. Review NHBRC coverage on any remaining primary stock; on resale, inspect body corporate health.
5. Register non-resident endorsement for repatriation on exit.

## What risks and red flags should Harbour Arch buyers watch?

Two things decide this purchase, and neither is the apartment. The first is the levy stack. An amenity-heavy precinct spreads pools, concierge and secured parking across its owners, and a 35-square-metre studio pays into that at close to the rate a 109-square-metre unit does, because much of the load is per-unit rather than per-square-metre. That is how a modelled 5% to 7% gross becomes 3.5% to 5.5% net on the smallest stock — the levy is a bigger deduction than the vacancy you were worrying about.

The second is the parking. Roughly 1,200 bays serve about 560 units, which means bays are not bundled into a flat by default; they are traded, priced and sometimes withheld. Never assume one comes with the unit. Read the bay number into the offer, price it separately, and check what it adds to the levy, because a studio let without parking in the Foreshore lets to a materially smaller pool of tenants.

And the calendar. Five more towers are scheduled to rise around you, which means construction noise, changing access, and a steady supply of newer stock competing with your resale for as long as the precinct is building out.

| --- | --- |
| Aggregator mislabels developer | Confirm Amdec and Tower 1 address on title |
| Levy shock on small studios | Model all-in carrying cost before yield |
| Precinct construction phase | Price Tower 1 vs future tower discounts |
| STR restrictions | Read conduct rules and hotel overlap |
| Parking not included | Verify bay number and cost in contract |
