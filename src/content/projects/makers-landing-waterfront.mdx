## What Makers Landing actually is

Makers Landing is a circular food economy precinct built by the V&A Waterfront with the National Treasury Jobs Fund: maker stations, co-op eateries, a fresh market and shared production kitchens rented by the day or month, inside about 3,000 m² of converted warehouse funded at around R63 million.

- Anchor tenants and co-op eateries alongside independent maker stations
- Shared production kitchens hired by the day or month by food SMEs
- Demo kitchens, events, and a 12 month business scale-up journey

That operating model creates hospitality employment and visitor footfall at the cruise terminal — valuable for landlords in Green Point and the V&A — but it does not issue title deeds to investors.

## Investor spillover to real residential

Makers Landing supports the case for nearby buy-to-let and lifestyle stock without adding a single unit to it. The R63 million precinct changes who walks the harbour edge, at what hours, and how a short-stay listing photographs, and those are real inputs to tenant appeal. What follows is the honest version of that spillover, stripped of the cruise-recovery optimism agents attach to it.

- Cruise passengers disembark into a curated food experience — positive for short-stay branding in Green Point and Waterfront-adjacent buildings.
- Jobs Fund narrative emphasises SMME job creation — modest support for long-let professional demand in the stadium corridor.
- Event and demo kitchen programming adds weekend footfall.

Spillover is amenity, not a yield guarantee, and the distinction matters when you are modelling a Green Point one-bedroom at about R2.2 million to R3.5 million on a modelled 8% gross. Footfall from a R63 million food precinct improves the walkable environment and the short-stay pitch. Footfall does not raise the rent a long-let tenant will sign for, and it does not reduce the levy or the municipal rates line. Build your base case on three live rental comparables within 500 metres of the building you are actually buying, then treat cruise-season programming as upside you have not paid for. Cruise arrivals are seasonal and set by global itinerary decisions no landlord controls, so an income assumption resting on terminal footfall is the first thing to fail in a weak season.

Most practically, the name attracts fabricated loft-apartment listings, and the cost of that spam falls on buyers who wire deposits before checking the erf.
