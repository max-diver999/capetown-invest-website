Quick answer: Station House is a Berman Brothers and D2E Properties development in Fresnaye that completed in 2022, launched from R1,595,000, and is now both sold out and fully let. Anyone searching for it is looking at a resale or a sublet, and the useful question is not the launch price but what the body corporate looks like four years on.

## What Station House is

A 2022 completion on the Atlantic Seaboard, delivered by two developers together.

| | |
| --- | --- |
| Location | Fresnaye, filed by the developer under Sea Point |
| Developers | Berman Brothers Group with D2E Properties |
| Completed | 2022 |
| Launched from | R1,595,000 |
| Unit range | Studio, one, two and three-bedroom, penthouse |
| Status | Sold out and fully let |

The outlook takes in the ocean, Lion's Head and the city, and the positioning is convenience rather than seclusion: a central address on the Seaboard at an entry price that, for that address, was notably accessible when it launched.

The joint venture is worth registering. A development delivered by two parties can carry a less obvious aftercare position than one delivered by a single developer, so on a resale it is worth asking who actually answers a defect query four years later.

## Both doors through the developer are shut

Sold out and fully let are two separate statements, and both are closed.

Sold out means the developer has no stock. Fully let means the developer's rental side has nothing either. So the routes in are a resale from an existing owner, or a sublet if one is released.

The financial consequence is the usual one on a completed scheme, and it runs the opposite way to what buyers expect. The developer sale carried VAT inside the price and no transfer duty. A private resale carries duty on the SARS scale plus the full conveyancing stack, so the route that closed was the cheaper one at the front end. The [transfer cost calculator](/guides/transfer-duty-calculator/) prices a resale at any figure, and the [off-plan guide](/guides/off-plan-property-cape-town-guide/) covers how cessions and resales work.

Insider tip: on a building that is fully let, approach the letting agents who manage units in it rather than watching portals. Stock in a well-tenanted block often changes hands before it is advertised.

## Fresnaye or Sea Point, and why the answer matters

Because the comparables differ, and the price follows the comparables.

Berman Brothers files the scheme under Sea Point and describes it as situated in Fresnaye. Both are honest. Fresnaye is the quieter residential slope above the Sea Point strip, and the two do not trade at the same level, so a valuation built on Sea Point comparables will not describe a Fresnaye apartment accurately.

Use the [Fresnaye page](/areas/fresnaye-property-investment/) for the local market and the [Sea Point page](/areas/sea-point-property-investment/) for the adjoining one, with the [Atlantic Seaboard guide](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) covering the corridor. ## What to check on a four-year-old building

Three documents, and they answer the question the brochure cannot.

1. **The last two AGM minutes**, which show what owners have actually argued about since handover.
2. **The current levy and its trajectory**, because a scheme that launched on a developer's projection has by now met its real costs.
3. **The reserve fund position**, which decides whether the next major maintenance item arrives as a special levy.

A fully let building tells you the units are desirable. It tells you nothing about whether the scheme has funded its roof. The [body corporate due diligence guide](/guides/body-corporate-due-diligence-cape-town/) sets out how to read all three, and it is the single highest-value hour in a resale purchase on the Seaboard.

## Advantages and disadvantages of buying here now

The building's maturity is both.

The advantages: a completed 2022 building with a demonstrated letting record rather than a projection, an established body corporate whose figures are real rather than forecast, and a Fresnaye address on the Atlantic Seaboard. The disadvantages: no developer stock, so supply is irregular and you wait for it; a resale cost stack that is heavier at the front end than the original sale was; and a joint-venture delivery that can complicate aftercare questions.

For a non-resident, the practical limit is finance rather than law: lenders generally stop near half the value for a foreign buyer, so the cash requirement on any Seaboard resale is substantial.
