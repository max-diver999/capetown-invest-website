## How to underwrite Silo District

Silo District is where Cape Town's harbour meets global art infrastructure. The converted Grain Silo hosts Zeitz MOCAA, and the surrounding towers — designed by VDMMA and allied teams — fragment public realm space to prioritise pedestrians over vehicles. For investors, Silo is the antithesis of a mass off-plan launch: tiny unit counts, high finish spec, and resale liquidity driven by international buyers who want museum precinct culture on their doorstep.

[Growthpoint Waterfront](/developers/growthpoint-waterfront/) and PIC stewardship matter because body corporate governance and precinct management stay institutional rather than boutique. That reduces surprise special levies relative to unknown developers, but it does not make the levies cheap. Waterfront sectional title often carries among the highest monthly charges in Cape Town, which compresses net yield even when gross rents look strong.

Pair this review with the [V&A Waterfront area guide](/areas/v-and-a-waterfront-property-investment/), [Green Point](/areas/green-point-property-investment/) spillover analysis, and the forward-looking [Granger Bay Waterfront](/projects/granger-bay-waterfront/) pipeline note.

## No 2 silo: the first residential silo

Thirty-one units is a very small body corporate by Cape Town standards, and that single number drives most of what matters here. With a scheme this size, each owner carries roughly one thirty-first of every communal cost, so a lift replacement, a façade repair, or a harbour-exposure waterproofing project lands hard rather than being diluted across hundreds of participation quotas. It also means one or two owners in arrears distort the whole levy roll. Pull the arrears schedule and the reserve fund balance, not just the monthly levy figure.

Completed in 2013 with one and two-bedroom formats and a rooftop pool, No 2 Silo was also among the first residential buildings in South Africa to carry a four-star Green Building Council rating. That was genuinely early, and it means the rating was awarded under a first-generation tool. Ask what the rating covered in practice: efficient services and envelope design usually translate to lower tenant utility bills, which supports retention when tariffs rise, but it says nothing about the levy itself.

For resale bidders, the practical constraint is supply. Thirty-one apartments produce very few transactions per year, so comparables are thin and pricing is set by whoever is in the market that month rather than by a deep run of recent sales. Expect to underwrite from precinct evidence and the [V&A Waterfront area view](/areas/v-and-a-waterfront-property-investment/) rather than from a clean set of in-building comps, and build the [body corporate review](/guides/body-corporate-due-diligence-cape-town/) into your offer timeline before you waive conditions.

## No 3 silo: scale and penthouse scarcity

No 3 Silo completed in 2017 with about 79 apartments, and the format range, from one-bedroom units up to five-bedroom penthouses, is what governs resale outcomes here. A scheme of 79 is more than twice the size of No 2 Silo's 31 units, so communal costs spread across more participation quotas and one owner in arrears carries roughly 1.3% of the levy roll rather than 3.2%. Depth of comparables is better at the smaller end, where one and two-bedroom units transact often enough to give a valuer something to price against. At the penthouse end there is effectively no series: a handful of units, each with a different footprint, view line, and terrace area, means every sale becomes its own negotiation. Ask the managing agent how many units of your exact size have transferred in the past 5 years, and how long each sat before it sold.

Format range also decides which buyer pool you exit into. One and two-bedroom units at No 3 compete directly with the [V&A Waterfront](/areas/v-and-a-waterfront-property-investment/) letting market and with [Green Point](/areas/green-point-property-investment/) stock a short walk away. Penthouses compete with nothing in particular, which cuts both ways on price.

## Pros, cons, and who Silo District suits

Silo District suits a buyer whose first objective is capital preservation in a recognised international address and whose second objective is income. Completed stock removes delivery risk entirely: both towers were handed over years ago, so there is no developer covenant to underwrite and no construction programme to police. What you take on instead is carry. Levies at the Waterfront rank among the highest in the metro, municipal rates run at roughly 0.69 cents per rand above the first R450,000 of value, and the two together are what pull a MODELED gross long-let of about 4% to 6% down to a MODELED net nearer 2.5% to 4.5%. Non-residents face no surcharge and can usually bond about half the purchase price locally, so the equity cheque is large and the income it earns is thin. Buyers who need the asset to pay for itself are reading about the wrong building.

## Waterfront levy reality check

The gap between gross and net at the Waterfront is wider than almost anywhere else in the metro, and levies do most of the damage. A Silo levy is not just building maintenance and insurance: it funds concierge, 24-hour manned security, precinct cleaning, plant and lift servicing on harbour-exposed equipment, and a share of the sustainability systems the towers were designed around. Salt air accelerates corrosion on façades, balustrades, and external plant, which shortens replacement cycles compared with an inland building of the same age.

Municipal rates compound it. City of Cape Town exempts the first R450,000 of value and then charges roughly 0.69 cents per rand per year, so an ultra-premium valuation produces an annual rates bill that a Century City apartment simply does not generate. That is a fixed cost independent of whether the unit is let.

Three checks before you bid:

- Ten-year maintenance plan and current reserve fund balance, since the STSMA requires the plan and its funding is what stands between you and a special levy.
- Special levy history for the past five years, including precinct-level charges raised before your ownership.
- Whether the levy is quoted inclusive or exclusive of utilities, parking bays, and storage.

Run the resulting number through the [levies guide](/guides/sectional-title-levies-cape-town/) and rebuild net yield from it. A trophy Waterfront asset can still be the right purchase, but only when you have priced the carry honestly rather than assuming a percentage borrowed from cheaper stock.

## Tourism, museums, and silo square events

Zeitz MOCAA is the reason this precinct reads as a cultural address rather than a harbour extension, and that has a measurable effect on who rents and who buys. A museum with international standing keeps foot traffic on Silo Square through the shoulder season, supports the restaurant and gallery tenancies at street level, and gives agents a story that travels well in London, Frankfurt, and New York. Buyers of trophy stock pay for that narrative.

The flip side is that you are buying a home inside a working visitor destination. Silo Square hosts events, and event days bring amplified sound, marquee build-up and strike, crowd flows, and restricted vehicle access. Lower floors and units facing the square carry more of this than upper units facing the port. Before you bid, ask the managing agent for the events calendar and for the precinct's noise and access protocol, then visit once on an ordinary weekday and once during an event or peak summer weekend.

Tourism density also shapes letting strategy. High visitor volume makes short-stay economically tempting, but Waterfront precinct rules and body corporate conduct rules often restrict it more tightly than a suburban block would, and City of Cape Town [short-term rental rules](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-cape-town/) sit on top of that. Confirm in writing what the scheme actually permits before you underwrite any nightly-rate scenario. If short-stay is off the table, your model has to work on long-let alone, which at this price point means accepting a low single-digit MODELED net.

## Gbcsa credentials and operating cost

No 2 Silo's four-star green rating was pioneering for residential in South Africa in 2013, signalling lower utility intensity for tenants and potential operating cost advantages versus unrated CBD towers. No 3 Silo extended sustainability thinking with façade and systems design suited to harbour wind and sun studies. Lower utilities do not automatically mean lower levies — Waterfront security, concierge, and precinct fees dominate — but they help tenant retention when electricity tariffs rise.

Resale buyers should ask whether major plant replacements are scheduled in the next five years and whether the body corporate has funded them in reserves. A special levy on a R12m Silo penthouse can exceed R200,000 per owner in a single year when lifts or façade maintenance cycle.

Institutional buyers sometimes benchmark Silo resales against London or Lisbon trophy flats on a price-per-square-metre basis; when sterling or euro strength favours rand entry, liquidity improves, but repatriation planning must be documented from day one through authorised dealer banks.

## No 2 versus No 3 buyer personas

The two towers attract different money for structural reasons, not marketing ones. No 2 Silo, completed in 2013 with 31 one and two-bedroom units, is the smaller ticket and the more residential of the pair. It suits a buyer who wants a genuine Waterfront pied-a-terre, uses it several weeks a year, and lets it long-term in between. The unit sizes are lettable to professionals and relocating executives, so there is an actual tenant pool at a rent that a real person pays out of salary.

No 3 Silo, completed in 2017 with around 79 units running up to five-bedroom penthouses, spans a much wider format range. The one-bedroom end behaves like No 2. The penthouse end does not: at that size and price the tenant pool thins to corporate lettings and a handful of individuals, and the asset becomes primarily a capital-preservation and owner-use decision. Yield stops being the metric that governs the purchase.

That difference should change your due diligence emphasis. In No 2, the small body corporate is the main risk, so reserves and arrears dominate. In No 3, format liquidity is the main risk, so ask how many units of your specific size have transacted in recent years and how long they sat. Neither tower is an income vehicle. If yield is the objective, the honest comparison is against inland stock such as [SkyWater Century City](/projects/skywater-century-city/) rather than against each other.
