## What off-plan diligence must La'Mare buyers complete before deposit?

La'Mare off-plan buyers must confirm NHBRC enrolment, deposit held in trust or bank guarantee, build milestones with penalty clauses, estimated levies stressed 20 percent above brochure, draft conduct rules on short-stay, and harbour noise and flood engineering sign-off before any reservation fee leaves your account.

Off-plan pricing at La'Mare works differently from resale, and the difference belongs in your deposit calculation. A new unit sold by a VAT-registered developer carries 15% VAT inside the price and attracts no transfer duty, so the R1.995m to R4.595m band is closer to an all-in number than a resale asking price is, and the cash you fund before the Q3 2027 handover is mostly deposit and guarantee rather than duty. What is not fixed is the levy: the scheme's roughly 84 apartments across seven blocks have no operating history, so the brochure figure is a projection and should be stressed at least 20% above it before any net yield means anything. Confirm NHBRC enrolment and that the deposit sits in trust or behind a bank guarantee first.

## Long-let versus short-stay at harbour

Hout Bay is a seasonal market in a way that Sea Point and the CBD are not. Summer fills the harbour, Chapman's Peak, and the beach; June through August the same stock competes on a much thinner tourism pool while the weather makes harbour-edge units a harder sell. That seasonality is the whole argument between the two letting models here.

Seasonality decides the letting model at La'Mare more than the finish or the view does. Long-let models roughly 5% to 6% gross on R2 million to R3 million tickets and compresses to about 3.5% to 4.5% net once levies, rates, and voids apply, a thinner net than Sea Point or the CBD produce but backed by a resident tenant base of families, harbour workers, and remote professionals. Short-stay looks stronger on a summer spreadsheet and weaker across a full year: rebuild it with 20% management, any body corporate cap on letting nights, and two winter void months, and the two models converge. A Q3 2027 handover also means the first full letting year opens on a summer season, which flatters any short-stay model built on that first year alone.

| Line | Long-let | Short-stay |
| --- | --- | --- |
| modelled gross | Near 5% to 6% | Summer-weighted, unproven off-plan |
| Management fee | 8% to 10% of rent | Near 20% plus platform cut |
| Occupancy risk | One tenant, one lease | Two likely winter void months |
| Setup cost | Minimal | Furnishing, linen, cleaning cycle |
| Rule risk | Standard conduct rules | Body corporate night caps |

Long-let compresses to roughly 3.5% to 4.5% net once levies, City of Cape Town rates, and voids come off, and that is the number to build your base case on. Short-stay can beat it in a strong season, but only if three conditions hold: the body corporate conduct rules permit tourism nights, you fund the furnishing and operating float, and you accept that your revenue concentrates into about five months.

For a scheme still targeting Q3 2027 handover, the conduct rules are draft documents and can tighten before the body corporate is constituted. Underwrite the long-let fallback and treat any short-stay upside as optional. The [short-term rental rules](/guides/short-term-rental-rules-cape-town/) guide covers what the City and a body corporate can each restrict.
