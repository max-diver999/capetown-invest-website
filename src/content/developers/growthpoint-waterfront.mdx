## How should you underwrite Growthpoint Waterfront?

Growthpoint is the institutional counterweight to Rabie's Century City income machine and Blok's Atlantic Seaboard infill. Listed on the JSE, Growthpoint Properties operates as co-owner of the [V&A Waterfront](/areas/v-and-a-waterfront-property-investment/) with the Public Investment Corporation, giving the precinct decades of capital markets discipline, sustainability reporting, and phased master planning. For foreign buyers, that matters because you are not buying a one-off boutique block; you are buying into a harbour asset class with hotel, retail, and tourism synergies baked into the address.

The investment case splits three ways. **Completed Silo District stock** is scarce resale sectional title beside Zeitz MOCAA with harbour and mountain view lines. **Marina and other Waterfront apartments** offer a similar trophy profile with different levy and aspect dynamics. **Granger Bay pipeline** is forward optionality: roughly 3.8 hectares and R24 billion of proposed mixed-use development between the existing Waterfront and [Green Point](/areas/green-point-property-investment/), with approvals targeted around late 2027 per public filings covered in our [Granger Bay news brief](/news/va-waterfront-granger-bay-development/).

This developer guide pairs with project reviews for [Silo District residences](/projects/silo-district-residences/) and [Granger Bay Waterfront](/projects/granger-bay-waterfront/), plus the [Atlantic Seaboard investment guide](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) for yield context.

## Silo District: art precinct meets residential scarcity

The Silo District wraps the converted Grain Silo that houses Zeitz MOCAA, with No 2 Silo, No 3 Silo, and commercial silos forming a fragmented public realm designed for pedestrians first. Residential towers emphasise sustainability: No 2 Silo achieved a 4-star Green Building Council rating as the first residential project assessed under early GBCSA tools. No 3 Silo adds larger formats up to five-bedroom penthouses with Silo Square frontage.

Silo District resale pricing rests on a very small pool. No 2 Silo holds 31 units completed in 2013, and No 3 Silo adds about 79 apartments completed in 2017, so barely more than 100 residential titles exist across the precinct core. Scarcity of that order means one motivated seller can reset the comparable set for a quarter, and it also means valuation per square metre is unreliable, because aspect, floor level, and Silo Square frontage move price more than floor area does. Ask the managing agent for the levy roll, the reserve fund balance, and any special levy resolved in the past three financial years, then read those against the precinct management contribution, which Waterfront schemes charge in addition to the ordinary body corporate levy. At these price points the marginal rand of purchase price attracts transfer duty at the top 13% band, so the all-in gap between two apartments that look similar on a listing page is wider than it appears.

Investors should treat Silo stock as **resale-only** with body corporate accounts driving net return more than harbour branding. Pull three recent sales and two active rentals for the same bedroom count before you model yield.

## Granger Bay pipeline and Green Point spillover

The proposed reclamation sits on the Green Point side of the existing precinct, which is why the suburb keeps surfacing in Waterfront sales conversations. The spillover argument runs that roughly 3.8 hectares of new mixed-use ground plus public promenade extends Waterfront foot traffic westward and pulls Beach Road stock into the precinct's pricing orbit.

Two things temper it. Timing is the first: parliamentary and environmental approvals are targeted around late 2027, and a reclamation behind new shore-protection breakwaters is a multi-year marine construction job before a single apartment is enrolled with the NHBRC. Anyone buying in [Green Point](/areas/green-point-property-investment/) today on Granger Bay logic is holding through a decade, not a cycle. Sequencing is the second: the construction period brings barge traffic, haul routes, and noise to the same shoreline the thesis says will improve. Owner-occupiers and short-let operators absorb that cost years before they see any benefit.

Approval risk is the part marketing tends to compress into a footnote. Reclaiming public coastline attracts objection from heritage, environmental, and public-access interests, and the scheme that eventually receives consent may be materially smaller than the one in the renders. Price the pipeline as optionality worth zero in your base case. The [Granger Bay project page](/projects/granger-bay-waterfront/) tracks what is actually approved versus proposed.

## What are the pros, cons and Growthpoint Waterfront suits?

Growthpoint Waterfront suits a narrow buyer profile, and the pros and cons below sort quickly by whether income matters to you. Capital preservation, precinct governance, and scarcity sit on one side; low MODELED net yield, high levies, and thin resale liquidity at the top price bands sit on the other. Read the list against your holding period, because most of the downside only bites on a short one.

**Pros**

- Institutional developer and PIC partnership reduce single-asset developer risk.
- Tourism, retail, and office depth support long-stay and corporate tenant demand.
- Silo District scarcity beside a global museum anchor.
- No foreign buyer surcharge; clean freehold sectional title path.
- Sustainability credentials support ESG-minded buyers.

**Cons**

- Ultra-premium pricing compresses MODELED net yield.
- High levies and municipal rates on Waterfront sectional title.
- Resale liquidity can be thin at the top price bands.
- Granger Bay remains approval-dependent.
- Competing hotel and short-stay stock adds supply in peak seasons.

**The trophy capital buyer** wants Waterfront address and museum precinct culture, accepting sub-5% modelled net.

**The foreign lifestyle owner** uses Silo or Marina stock for seasonal use with long-let fallback; verify body corporate short-stay rules.

**The income investor** should compare [Century City](/guides/century-city-property-investment-guide/) Rabie stock before paying Waterfront premiums.

**The pipeline speculator** must price Granger Bay as optional upside after late-2027 approvals, not base-case rent growth.

## Due diligence documents unique to Waterfront stock

Waterfront due diligence adds documents that an ordinary sectional title purchase never produces. Request the latest audited body corporate financials, the precinct management budget and the scheme's share of it, proof of GBCSA or equivalent green ratings where they are used in marketing, and any marina, berth, or harbour view easement that could affect future sightlines. For Granger Bay pipeline material, demand written confirmation of which approvals exist today against which are projected for late 2027, because a reclamation of roughly 3.8 hectares with a budget near R24 billion can be consented in a smaller form than the renders show. Keep deposits with the conveyancer until every condition precedent clears, and record inbound funds through your bank so the deal receipt trail matches exchange control expectations. Two documents decide net yield here: the levy roll and the precinct management contribution, and only the first usually reaches a buyer unprompted.

## Marina, cruise terminal, and Makers Landing spillover

The V&A is still a working harbour, and that shapes residential performance more than most offshore buyers expect. Marina stock sits on canal and basin frontage where the amenity is water access and berth views, and where your neighbour may be operating a vessel rather than letting an apartment.

Cruise terminal calls concentrate visitor volume into defined windows instead of spreading it across the year. That suits retail and hospitality tenants and does little for a long-let landlord, whose tenant experiences the same weeks as congestion and parking pressure. If your model leans on short-stay rates, map the call season against your peak-rate assumptions rather than assuming a year-round premium.

[Makers Landing](/projects/makers-landing-waterfront/), the food and culture space on the terminal side, is the clearest expression of the precinct's placemaking approach: small operators, local production, and a reason for residents to walk east rather than straight into the mall. Amenity of that kind supports resale narrative and tenant retention, but it rarely shows up as a rent line you can defend in a valuation.

The practical checks are unglamorous. Confirm your aspect against harbour operations, ask specifically about night-time noise from vessel movement and terminal logistics, and read the precinct management contribution separately from the body corporate levy, because Waterfront schemes carry both and only one appears in most listings.

## Listed REIT transparency for offshore shareholders

One under-used advantage of a listed co-owner is that much of what a private developer keeps confidential is published. Growthpoint Properties reports to the JSE, so annual and interim results, SENS announcements, and segmental disclosure carry V&A Waterfront occupancy, trading density, capital expenditure, and pipeline commentary. Before offering on Silo or Marina resale, read the parent company's own description of the precinct rather than the sales brochure. Where the two disagree, the audited document wins.

Two boundaries are worth keeping clear. Reported precinct performance is retail, office, and hotel performance. It tells you about footfall and tenant health, which support your address over time, but it is not evidence about achievable sectional title rent for your specific unit. Body corporate financials remain the only document that speaks to net yield, and those sit outside the REIT's reporting entirely.

The second boundary is asset choice. Buying Growthpoint shares and buying an apartment inside the precinct are different trades that happen to share a name. The shares offer daily liquidity, diversification across a national portfolio, and distributions subject to South African withholding, often reduced under a double tax agreement with your country of residence. The apartment offers control, personal use, and local leverage up to 50% loan-to-value for non-residents, with none of the daily pricing and considerably slower exit. Some investors discover after transfer that they wanted the first and bought the second.
