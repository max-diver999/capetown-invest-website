## How should investors read Amdec Harbour Arch versus Foreshore alternatives?

Harbour Arch is Amdec's R16 billion, 5.8 hectare Foreshore precinct: six towers, about 1,500 apartments and 500 Marriott hotel keys on the old Culemborg site. Against single-tower CBD conversions, the developer sells scale and amenity rather than yield, with Tower 1 stock launched between R2.1 million and R8 million.

For international buyers comparing [Foreshore Place](/projects/foreshore-place-cape-town/), [Growthpoint Waterfront](/developers/growthpoint-waterfront/), and City Bowl stock, Amdec sits in the gateway CBD bucket: professional tenants, semigration, and capital preservation more than student yield.

This developer guide covers track record, Harbour Arch phasing, The Yacht Club, how Amdec prices and sells towers, and the diligence checklist foreign buyers still need despite the brand.

## National pedigree: Melrose Arch to Harbour Arch

Melrose Arch is fair evidence of Amdec's placemaking and poor evidence of price growth, because it matured over two decades in another metro. The harder local data point is Tower 1, an 80,000 m² mixed-use block carrying about 560 apartments that ran a Covid-era programme and handed over from May 2023.

Galleria retail, a managed public realm and hotel keys sitting inside a residential precinct all travelled from Johannesburg to the Foreshore. Capital performance did not: what Melrose Arch did for a Johannesburg one-bedroom tells you nothing about a Foreshore resale. Treat the pedigree as evidence of execution capacity.

What the pedigree does justify checking is delivery discipline. Tower 1 ran through a Covid-era build programme and handed over from May 2023 with about 560 apartments, which is a real data point on the group's ability to finish an 80,000 m² mixed-use block. Ask for the practical completion date against the original contractual date, the NHBRC enrolment number for the specific tower you are buying, and the snag resolution record from Tower 1 owners rather than from the sales suite. The [NHBRC warranty guide](/guides/nhbrc-warranty-south-africa-new-build/) sets out what that cover actually pays for.

The group's Western Cape base at Steenberg Office Park in Tokai matters more than the Johannesburg history once something goes wrong after handover. Warranty claims, defect rectification, and body corporate handover disputes are handled locally. Confirm which legal entity carries the NHBRC obligation on your tower, because the developing company and the group brand are rarely the same person on paper.

## Harbour Arch precinct layout

The finished precinct is planned around six towers and roughly 200,000 m² of offices, apartments, urban park, shopping galleria, an eighth-floor open-air piazza, two Marriott hotels with about 500 keys, and a flagship fitness club. Tower 1 alone carries about 560 apartments and 1,200 parking bays across some 80,000 m².

Tower 1 mixes those apartments with ground-floor restaurants and high-end vehicle retail. Pricing from about R2.1 million to R8 million places Amdec between mid-Foreshore conversions and ultra-prime Waterfront resales.

## Who should buy Amdec Harbour Arch or Yacht Club stock?

Harbour Arch and The Yacht Club suit two buyers and disappoint a third. The precinct works for a semigration owner-occupier who wants a secure CBD base with hotel, gym and piazza on site, and for a landlord underwriting corporate lets at the R2.1 million to R8 million entry range. It works badly for anyone needing a quick exit, because Towers 2 to 6 keep the site under construction for years.

- Landmark address and Melrose Arch track record.
- Tower 1 delivered on programme post-Covid.
- Strong initial sell-through on launch tranche.
- Hotel and retail placemaking supports long-term amenity.
- No foreign buyer surcharge.

**Cons**

- Foreshore pipeline dense — rent growth not guaranteed.

## Tower phasing and construction noise

Harbour Arch remains a construction site for years after Tower 1 occupation, because Towers 2 to 6 add crane cycles, road diversions and dust that reach short-let reviews and owner satisfaction. Amdec starts Tower 2 once Tower 1 is substantially let, so price that friction into a hold of 7 to 10 years.

That sequencing reduces empty-precinct risk but extends the skyline construction window. Long-hold investors can live with it; flip investors cannot, and resale buyers in Tower 1 inherit the same cranes.

## Levy and parking economics on Foreshore towers

Two line items decide whether a Foreshore apartment clears its costs, and neither is on a launch price list: the levy, apportioned by participation quota, and parking, where roughly 1,200 bays serve about 560 Tower 1 apartments. Levies model at R3,800 to R5,200 a month before a bay is added.

A larger section carries a larger share of lift maintenance, standby generation, concierge, and shared amenity whether or not the owner ever uses the piazza or the fitness club. Amenity-heavy precincts run structurally higher levies than a plain residential block, and Harbour Arch is amenity-heavy by design.

On parking, bays are not automatically bundled one-to-one with sections. Establish in writing whether your bay is a separate section on the title deed, an exclusive use area registered against the scheme, or a monthly hire arrangement the body corporate can reprice at will. Only the first two survive a resale intact, and the third quietly transfers pricing power to the trustees.

Municipal rates sit outside the levy entirely and are billed to the owner, not the scheme. The City exempts the first R450,000 of valuation and charges roughly 0.69 cents per rand per year above that.

Before deposit, request the last two years of audited body corporate financials, the reserve fund balance, and the trustee minutes. Under the STSMA a special resolution needs 75% support, so a special levy of any scale appears in those minutes well before it appears on your statement. The [sectional title levy guide](/guides/sectional-title-levies-cape-town/) covers how to read the budget line by line.

## Foreign buyer currency and bond workflow

Non-residents may finance up to 50% of the purchase price with a South African bond, so the R2.1 million Harbour Arch entry ticket requires roughly R1.05 million of onshore debt against R1.05 million of offshore equity, before transfer duty, bond registration and furnishing for a corporate let.

Transfer duty on R2.1 million falls in the 6% band that starts at R1,663,800, since the first R1,210,000 of any price is zero-rated, so budget that cheque from the offshore side rather than assuming the bond stretches to cover it. On exit the arithmetic reverses: section 35A obliges the conveyancer to withhold 7.5% of the price from a non-resident individual seller, 10% from a company and 15% from a trust, on any sale above R2 million. Holding through a trust or company also lifts the CGT inclusion rate from 40% to 80%.

Record FICA and exchange-control paperwork when funds land: your future repatriation on sale depends on clean inward flows.
