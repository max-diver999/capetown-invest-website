## What kind of developer is this?

A place developer. The work concentrates on the Atlantic Seaboard, the group describes itself as a leading developer on that coast, and the stated history runs to about 35 years. Named schemes include Mont Reve in Sea Point, The Estate and Station House, alongside a luxury partnership operating as Gallery Group Properties.

That is a meaningfully different model from the two other developer types this site covers, and the differences show up at resale rather than at purchase.

| | Place developer | Master developer | Volume developer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Example | Berman Brothers, Atlantic Seaboard | [Rabie](/developers/rabie-property-developers/), Century City | [Balwin](/developers/balwin-properties/), multiple locations |
| Where it builds | One coastline, many small sites | One precinct it planned | Many locations, one product |
| What you compete with at resale | The wider suburb | The precinct's next phase | The scheme's next phase |
| What the record signals | Local knowledge and reputation | Delivery and precinct control | Process and scale |

Each model produces a different exit. On the Atlantic Seaboard a buyer competes with whatever else happens to be listed that month in a supply-constrained suburb, rather than with a pipeline the developer controls.

## What does 35 years on one coast tell you?

That the buildings exist and can be inspected, which is a stronger signal than any brochure. A developer working the same eight suburbs for three and a half decades has left a physical record within a short drive, and the useful diligence is to visit it.

Two things a long single-market record supports:

1. **Delivery risk is lower.** A developer that has completed schemes repeatedly in the same market has cleared the same approvals, the same geotechnical conditions and the same municipal processes many times.
2. **Reputational exposure is concentrated.** A boutique developer with a handful of schemes on one coast cannot afford a bad one, because there is nowhere for the reputation to hide. That aligns interests more tightly than scale does.

Two things it does not support. It says nothing about the specific scheme's funding, and it says nothing about the founding levy your body corporate will inherit. A well-regarded developer can still set a first-year levy that proves unrealistic, and that lands on owners rather than on the developer.

Insider tip: walk two completed schemes and speak to an owner in each, not the sales agent. Ask two questions: how close the handover date came to the promised one, and how defects were handled in the first year. Those two answers tell you more about a developer than any award or anniversary.

## What does the Atlantic Seaboard context add?

Constraint, which is the coast's defining commercial fact. The Atlantic Seaboard is bounded by mountain on one side and sea on the other, so new supply arrives as small buildings rather than as phases, and the eight suburbs on it carry the highest prices per square metre in the city.

That has a direct effect on a new-build purchase here. Scarcity supports value, and it also means a single scheme is a larger share of the year's new supply in its suburb than it would be anywhere else. The [Atlantic Seaboard guide](/guides/atlantic-seaboard-property-investment-guide/) ranks the eight suburbs on modelled yield, which runs from about 7.5% net in Sea Point down to roughly 3.5% in Llandudno, and the [Sea Point page](/areas/sea-point-property-investment/) covers the suburb where Mont Reve sits in detail, including the draft short-term letting by-law that reaches that suburb before any other.

Sea Point is worth a specific note. It is the densest residential node on the coast and the one with the best modelled yield on it, at about 9.7% gross and 7.5% net on a one-bedroom, and it is also the suburb most exposed to a change in nightly-letting rules. A buyer underwriting a Sea Point new build should model the long lease as the floor rather than the short-let upside as the case.

## What to establish before you offer

1. **NHBRC enrolment naming your unit**, not the developer's company registration.
2. **The deposit held in a named attorney trust account**, with the attorney named in the agreement.
3. **The founding body corporate budget**, and whether the first-year levy is realistic against comparable buildings in the same suburb.
4. **The specification schedule for your unit**, in writing, including what is provisional.
5. **Two completed schemes visited**, with an owner conversation in each.
6. **The scheme's conduct rules on letting**, since a body corporate can restrict nightly letting by 75% special resolution and Sea Point is where that matters most.
